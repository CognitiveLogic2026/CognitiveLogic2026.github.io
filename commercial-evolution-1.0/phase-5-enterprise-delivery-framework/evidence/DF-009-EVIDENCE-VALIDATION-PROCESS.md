# Purpose

This document defines the enterprise framework governing the validation of evidence collected during Cognitive Logic assessments.

The framework establishes a standardized, explainable, auditable and technology-independent validation process to determine whether collected evidence is sufficiently reliable, authentic, complete, consistent and traceable to support governance decisions.

The framework applies to every assessment performed within the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework and complements the Evidence Collection Framework (DF-008) by governing all activities performed after evidence has been collected and before assessment conclusions are approved.

The process defined in this document is provider-independent, technology-independent and fully aligned with the Cognitive Logic Sovereign Intelligence Principle (ADR-CLE-004).
# Objectives

The objectives of this framework are to:

- establish a standardized enterprise evidence validation process;
- ensure evidence quality before assessment conclusions are produced;
- define objective validation criteria applicable across all services;
- guarantee evidence authenticity and integrity;
- evaluate evidence reliability and confidence;
- ensure evidence sufficiency for decision-making;
- identify inconsistencies and contradictory evidence;
- define validation responsibilities and approval authorities;
- provide complete validation traceability;
- support explainable and auditable governance decisions;
- enable repeatable validation practices across engagements;
- establish continuous improvement mechanisms for evidence validation.

# Scope

This framework applies to every Cognitive Logic assessment, including but not limited to:

- AI Governance Assessments;
- Knowledge Governance Assessments;
- AI Act Readiness Assessments;
- Explainability Assessments;
- Enterprise Risk Assessments;
- Organizational Assessments;
- Executive Advisory engagements;
- Governance maturity evaluations.

The framework governs every validation activity performed after evidence collection and before analytical conclusions, recommendations, risk evaluations or governance decisions are formally approved.

| Framework | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|

The two frameworks operate as complementary components of the Enterprise Delivery Framework while maintaining clear separation of responsibilities.
# Validation Principles

Evidence validation shall always be governed by the following enterprise principles.

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Independence | Validation activities shall remain independent from evidence generation whenever feasible. |
| Objectivity | Validation decisions shall be based exclusively on verifiable evidence. |
| Evidence First | Conclusions shall never precede validated evidence. |
| Explainability | Every validation outcome shall be understandable and documented. |
| Traceability | Every validation decision shall be fully traceable to its supporting evidence. |
| Repeatability | Equivalent evidence shall produce equivalent validation outcomes. |
| Proportionality | Validation effort shall be proportional to assessment risk and impact. |
| Completeness | Validation shall consider all relevant available evidence. |
| Consistency | Validation criteria shall be applied uniformly across assessments. |
| Accountability | Every validation decision shall have an identified responsible role. |
| Auditability | Validation activities shall be reviewable by internal and external auditors. |
| Technology Independence | Validation shall never depend on specific technologies or vendors. |

# Validation Governance

Evidence validation represents a formal governance activity within the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

Validation activities shall ensure that assessment outputs are supported only by evidence meeting the required quality standards before analytical conclusions, recommendations or governance decisions are approved.

Validation governance shall operate according to ADR-CLE-004 and shall preserve the integrity, explainability and trustworthiness of the entire assessment lifecycle.
# Validation Governance Structure

| Governance Component | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Assessment Lead | Overall accountability for evidence validation activities |
| Evidence Validator | Performs evidence validation according to this framework |
| Domain Expert | Reviews domain-specific evidence when required |
| Quality Reviewer | Verifies methodological compliance |
| Executive Approver | Grants final approval when validation is complete |

# Validation Prerequisites

Evidence validation shall begin only when all of the following conditions are satisfied.

| Prerequisite | Description |
|---|---|
| Evidence Collection Completed | Evidence has been collected according to DF-008. |
| Evidence Traceability Available | Every evidence item is uniquely identifiable. |
| Assessment Scope Defined | Validation boundaries are documented. |
| Validation Criteria Established | Applicable validation criteria are available before execution. |
| Required Reviewers Assigned | Validation responsibilities have been formally allocated. |
# Validation Lifecycle

Evidence validation shall follow a structured lifecycle to ensure consistency, repeatability and governance across every assessment.

| Phase | Objective | Primary Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preparation | Define validation scope, criteria and responsibilities | Validation Plan |
| 2. Initial Review | Verify completeness of collected evidence | Initial Validation Record |
| 3. Authenticity Verification | Confirm evidence origin and legitimacy | Authenticity Assessment |
| 4. Integrity Verification | Verify that evidence has not been altered or corrupted | Integrity Assessment |
| 5. Reliability Assessment | Evaluate credibility and trustworthiness | Reliability Rating |
| 6. Consistency Assessment | Verify internal and cross-source consistency | Consistency Report |
| 7. Sufficiency Assessment | Determine whether available evidence supports conclusions | Sufficiency Decision |
| 8. Conflict Resolution | Resolve contradictory or inconsistent evidence | Conflict Resolution Record |
| 9. Final Validation | Consolidate validation outcomes | Validation Report |
| 10. Approval | Obtain formal validation approval | Approved Validation Record |

# Validation Workflow

The validation workflow shall ensure that every evidence item progresses through a controlled sequence of review activities before it can support assessment conclusions.

No evidence shall be considered validated unless all mandatory validation phases have been completed or formally exempted through an approved exception process.

Validation activities shall be documented throughout the lifecycle to preserve explainability, traceability and auditability.
# Validation Criteria

Each evidence item shall be evaluated against a common set of enterprise validation criteria before it can be accepted for assessment purposes.

| Validation Criterion | Objective | Typical Evaluation Question |
|---|---|---|
| Authenticity | Verify that the evidence originates from a legitimate source | Is the origin demonstrable? |
| Integrity | Verify that the evidence has not been altered | Has the evidence remained unchanged? |
| Reliability | Assess the credibility of the source | Can the source reasonably be trusted? |
| Accuracy | Evaluate factual correctness | Is the information factually correct? |
| Completeness | Verify that the evidence contains sufficient information | Are essential elements present? |
| Consistency | Verify alignment with other validated evidence | Does it conflict with other evidence? |
| Relevance | Confirm alignment with assessment objectives | Does it support the assessment scope? |
| Timeliness | Verify that the evidence remains current | Is the evidence still applicable? |
| Traceability | Confirm complete linkage to its origin | Can the evidence lifecycle be reconstructed? |

# Acceptance Criteria

Evidence should normally be accepted when all of the following conditions are satisfied.

| Acceptance Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Authentic source confirmed | The origin of the evidence has been verified. |
| Integrity preserved | No unauthorized alteration has been identified. |
| Reliable source | The source satisfies the required reliability threshold. |
| Relevant to scope | The evidence directly supports the assessment objectives. |
| Consistent with validated evidence | No unresolved contradictions remain. |
| Sufficient quality | The evidence satisfies the defined quality expectations. |
| Fully traceable | Complete traceability information is available. |

# Rejection Criteria

Evidence should normally be rejected or returned for further review when one or more of the following conditions apply.

| Rejection Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Unknown origin | Source cannot be verified. |
| Integrity cannot be demonstrated | Alteration cannot be excluded. |
| Insufficient reliability | Source credibility is below acceptable levels. |
| Material inconsistency | Significant unresolved conflicts exist. |
| Insufficient information | Evidence is incomplete for validation purposes. |
| Outside assessment scope | Evidence is not relevant to the assessment objectives. |
| Missing traceability | Required traceability information is unavailable. |
# Evidence Sufficiency Assessment

Evidence sufficiency determines whether the validated evidence collectively supports reliable assessment conclusions.

Sufficiency shall always be evaluated at assessment level rather than solely at individual evidence level.

| Sufficiency Level | Description | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient | Evidence does not adequately support conclusions | Additional evidence required |
| Partially Sufficient | Evidence supports only part of the assessment | Extend evidence collection |
| Sufficient | Evidence adequately supports assessment conclusions | Proceed |
| Highly Sufficient | Multiple independent evidence sources confirm conclusions | Proceed with high confidence |

# Evidence Reliability Assessment

Evidence reliability shall be assessed using objective evaluation criteria.

| Reliability Level | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Very High | Independent, authoritative, directly verifiable source |
| High | Reliable source with minor limitations |
| Moderate | Credible source requiring corroboration |
| Low | Weak source requiring significant additional validation |
| Unacceptable | Reliability cannot be demonstrated |

# Evidence Confidence Assessment

Confidence represents the degree of trust that can reasonably be placed in validated evidence after the completion of the validation process.

| Confidence Level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Very High | Evidence strongly supports assessment conclusions |
| High | Conclusions are well supported with limited uncertainty |
| Moderate | Conclusions are acceptable but additional corroboration would strengthen confidence |
| Low | Significant uncertainty remains |
| Very Low | Evidence should not support governance decisions |

# Evidence Corroboration

Whenever reasonably possible, significant findings should be supported by multiple independent evidence sources.

Corroboration may include:

- independent interviews;
- organizational documentation;
- policies and procedures;
- operational records;
- direct observations;
- governance artefacts;
- system-generated reports;
- independent expert reviews.

Greater corroboration increases evidence confidence, improves explainability and strengthens auditability without replacing professional judgement.
# Handling Contradictory Evidence

Contradictory evidence shall not be discarded without documented evaluation.

Whenever conflicting evidence is identified, the validation process shall determine whether the inconsistency originates from:

- different observation periods;
- different organizational contexts;
- incomplete information;
- methodological differences;
- unreliable sources;
- data quality issues;
- interpretation differences.

Every identified contradiction shall be documented together with the rationale supporting the final validation decision.

| Step | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify conflicting evidence | Conflict Record |
| 2 | Assess materiality | Materiality Assessment |
| 3 | Review source reliability | Reliability Review |
| 4 | Seek corroborating evidence | Corroboration Record |
| 5 | Consult domain experts when required | Expert Review |
| 6 | Determine resolution | Resolution Decision |
| 7 | Document rationale | Validation Documentation |
| 8 | Obtain approval when required | Approved Resolution |

# Validation Documentation

Every validation activity shall be documented to ensure explainability, repeatability and auditability.

The validation record should include, at minimum:

| Validation Record Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Validation identifier | Unique traceability |
| Evidence references | Link to validated evidence |
| Validation criteria applied | Explain evaluation basis |
| Validation results | Record assessment outcome |
| Identified issues | Document exceptions and findings |
| Conflict resolution | Record handling of contradictions |
| Validation rationale | Explain decisions |
| Reviewer identification | Accountability |
| Approval information | Governance traceability |
| Validation date | Audit history |

# Validation Traceability

Every validation decision shall remain fully traceable throughout the entire assessment lifecycle.

Traceability shall enable auditors and reviewers to reconstruct:

- the evidence that was validated;
- the validation criteria applied;
- the validation activities performed;
- the individuals responsible;
- approvals obtained;
- exceptions granted;
- supporting documentation;
- the relationship between validated evidence and final assessment conclusions.
# Validation Approvals

Validation activities shall be formally approved before validated evidence may be used to support assessment findings, recommendations or governance decisions.

| Approval Level | Typical Responsibility | Approval Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Operational Approval | Assessment Lead | Routine validation activities |
| Quality Approval | Quality Reviewer | Methodological compliance |
| Executive Approval | Executive Approver | High-impact or strategic assessments |

No assessment shall proceed to final reporting unless the required validation approvals have been completed or formally waived through an approved exception process.

# Validation Exceptions

Validation exceptions shall be documented, justified and approved according to established governance procedures.

Typical exceptions may include:

- unavailable historical evidence;
- externally controlled documentation;
- time-constrained assessments;
- organizational limitations;
- regulatory restrictions.

Each exception shall include documented justification, associated risks, mitigation actions and approval information.

# Validation Escalation

Material validation issues shall be escalated whenever they may significantly affect assessment reliability.

| Escalation Trigger | Typical Action |
|---|---|
| Missing critical evidence | Suspend validation until resolved |
| Unresolved contradictory evidence | Escalate for governance review |
| Evidence authenticity cannot be demonstrated | Executive review required |
| Integrity concerns | Immediate investigation |
| Major methodological disagreement | Independent quality review |

# Quality Controls

Quality controls shall ensure that validation activities remain consistent, objective and repeatable across all engagements.

Typical quality controls include:

- peer review;
- quality assurance review;
- methodological compliance verification;
- traceability verification;
- completeness review;
- documentation review;
- approval verification;
- periodic internal audits.

# Continuous Improvement

The Evidence Validation Process shall be periodically reviewed to improve effectiveness, consistency and governance maturity.

Continuous improvement activities should consider:

- lessons learned from completed assessments;
- audit findings;
- recurring validation issues;
- stakeholder feedback;
- updates to enterprise governance methodologies;
- regulatory evolution;
- improvements identified during quality assurance reviews.

Improvements shall be documented, evaluated and incorporated through the Enterprise Delivery Framework governance process.

# Conclusion

Evidence validation represents a fundamental governance activity within the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.

By establishing standardized validation principles, responsibilities, controls and approval mechanisms, this framework ensures that assessment conclusions are supported by evidence that is authentic, reliable, sufficient, explainable, auditable and fully traceable.

The framework operates in conjunction with DF-008 while remaining fully aligned with ADR-CLE-004 and the Evidence First principle that underpins the Cognitive Logic methodology.
