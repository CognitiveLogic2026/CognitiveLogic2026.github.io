# 1. Purpose

The KPI Measurement Framework defines the enterprise methodology governing the design, definition, measurement, normalization, monitoring, validation, reporting, review, and continuous improvement of Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) used throughout Cognitive Logic assessment services.

The framework establishes a technology-independent, provider-independent, explainable, traceable, evidence-based, and auditable measurement model applicable across all enterprise assessment activities.
# 2. Scope

This framework governs the complete lifecycle of enterprise KPIs adopted within Cognitive Logic delivery methodologies.

It applies to all measurement activities performed during:

- AI Governance Assessments
- Knowledge Governance Assessments
- AI Act Readiness Assessments
- Explainability Assessments
- Enterprise Assessments
- Organizational Assessments
- Risk Assessments
- Future Cognitive Logic assessment services

The framework defines the methodological rules for KPI management independently of implementation technologies, software platforms, databases, reporting tools, visualization systems, or analytical products.

# 4. KPI Governance Principles

The KPI Measurement Framework is governed by enterprise principles ensuring that every indicator is measurable, explainable, evidence-based, and suitable for decision support.

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Evidence First | Every KPI shall be supported by verifiable evidence. |
| Governance First | KPI management shall follow defined governance responsibilities and approval processes. |
| Measurement First | Decisions shall rely on measurable indicators whenever possible. |
| Explainability | KPI definitions, calculations and interpretations shall be understandable. |
| Traceability | Every KPI shall be traceable to its source, calculation method and supporting evidence. |
| Consistency | Equivalent conditions shall produce equivalent measurements. |
| Objectivity | KPI evaluation shall minimize subjective interpretation. |
| Repeatability | Independent assessors shall be able to reproduce the same measurement process. |
| Auditability | KPI calculations and assumptions shall be available for audit. |
| Quality | KPI data shall satisfy predefined quality criteria before use. |
| Proportionality | KPI complexity shall remain appropriate to assessment objectives. |
| Technology Independence | KPI methodology shall remain independent from software platforms, vendors and implementation technologies. |

The principles defined in this framework are aligned with the Cognitive Logic governance model and the Sovereign Intelligence Principle established by ADR-CLE-004.
# 5. KPI Lifecycle

Every KPI shall follow a controlled lifecycle to ensure consistency, quality, governance, and continuous improvement throughout its operational use.

| Phase | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Business Need Identification | Identify the decision or governance objective requiring measurement. |
| KPI Definition | Define the KPI, its purpose, scope, formula, and expected outcome. |
| Design Review | Verify methodological consistency and alignment with governance principles. |
| Baseline Establishment | Determine the initial reference value for future comparisons. |
| Target Definition | Establish expected performance levels and acceptable thresholds. |
| Measurement | Collect and calculate KPI values using the approved methodology. |
| Validation | Verify completeness, accuracy, consistency, and supporting evidence. |
| Reporting | Present KPI results to stakeholders in a clear and explainable manner. |
| Review | Evaluate KPI effectiveness, relevance, and continued suitability. |
| Improvement | Update definitions, targets, thresholds, or methodologies when justified through governance. |
| Retirement | Formally decommission obsolete or superseded KPIs while preserving historical traceability. |

No KPI shall enter operational use before completing the definition, review, and approval phases.

## 7.2 KPI Definition Principles

A KPI definition shall:

- support a clearly identified business objective;
- measure a single, well-defined concept;
- use objective and reproducible measurement criteria;
- specify the required evidence for validation;
- define ownership and accountability;
- establish baseline, targets, thresholds, and tolerances;
- remain understandable by independent reviewers;
- support full traceability and auditability throughout its lifecycle.
# 8. Measurement Methodology

The KPI Measurement Framework establishes a standardized methodology to ensure that all measurements are objective, consistent, explainable, repeatable, and comparable across different assessments.

## 8.1 Measurement Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Objectivity | Measurements shall rely on observable and verifiable information. |
| Consistency | Equivalent conditions shall produce equivalent results. |
| Repeatability | Independent assessors shall be able to reproduce measurements using the approved methodology. |
| Comparability | Results shall remain comparable across assessments and reporting periods. |
| Completeness | Measurements shall include all required inputs and supporting evidence. |
| Transparency | Measurement methods shall be fully documented and understandable. |

---

## 8.2 Measurement Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Measurement Unit | Unit used to express the KPI value. |
| Baseline | Initial reference used for comparison. |
| Target Value | Expected performance level. |
| Threshold | Boundary defining acceptable performance. |
| Tolerance | Permitted deviation before corrective action is required. |
| Measurement Frequency | Approved interval for measurement. |
| Confidence Level | Qualitative assessment of measurement reliability where applicable. |

---

## 8.3 Indicator Types

| Indicator Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Quantitative Indicator | Measured using numerical values. |
| Qualitative Indicator | Measured using structured assessment criteria. |
| Leading Indicator | Predicts future performance or emerging conditions. |
| Lagging Indicator | Measures achieved outcomes or historical performance. |

---

## 8.4 Normalization Principles

Normalization shall be applied whenever KPI values originating from different scales, units, or measurement methods need to be compared or aggregated.

The normalization methodology shall be documented, repeatable, and consistently applied throughout the assessment.

---

## 8.5 Weighting Principles

Weighting shall be applied only when justified by documented business objectives or governance requirements.

Weighting criteria shall be:

- objective;
- documented;
- approved through governance;
- consistently applied;
- periodically reviewed.

---

## 8.6 Aggregation Rules

Aggregated KPI results shall:

- preserve traceability to individual measurements;
- document aggregation logic;
- maintain explainability;
- avoid distortion of underlying evidence;
- support independent verification.
# 9. Scoring Framework

The Scoring Framework defines the enterprise methodology used to transform KPI measurements into consistent, explainable, and decision-supporting assessment results.

The framework establishes common scoring principles without prescribing service-specific scoring models.

## 9.1 Scoring Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| Standardization | Ensure consistent interpretation of KPI results. |
| Comparability | Enable comparison across assessments and reporting periods. |
| Explainability | Ensure every score can be understood and justified. |
| Traceability | Preserve linkage between scores, calculations, and evidence. |
| Decision Support | Provide meaningful information for governance decisions. |

---

## 9.2 Scoring Principles

Scoring methodologies shall:

- be documented;
- be reproducible;
- be evidence-based;
- avoid arbitrary evaluations;
- preserve measurement transparency;
- support independent verification.

---

## 9.3 Performance Levels

| Performance Level | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Critical | Performance requires immediate attention. |
| Needs Improvement | Performance is below expected objectives. |
| Acceptable | Performance satisfies minimum expectations. |
| Good | Performance exceeds minimum expectations. |

---

Every reported score shall include:

- KPI identification;
- measured value;
- applicable baseline;
- target value;
- applicable thresholds;

# 10. KPI Ownership and Responsibilities

Effective KPI governance requires clearly assigned ownership, accountability, review responsibilities, and approval authorities throughout the KPI lifecycle.

| Role | Primary Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| KPI Owner | Defines, maintains and ensures the ongoing relevance of the KPI. |
| Assessment Lead | Ensures correct application of KPI methodologies during assessments. |
| Assessor | Performs measurements according to the approved methodology. |
| Evidence Reviewer | Verifies that supporting evidence satisfies validation requirements. |
| Quality Reviewer | Verifies methodological consistency and measurement quality. |
| Governance Authority | Approves KPI definitions, significant modifications and retirement decisions. |

---

## 10.1 Responsibility Principles

Responsibilities shall ensure:

- clear accountability for every KPI;
- segregation between measurement and validation activities;
- documented approval of KPI definitions and changes;
- traceability of ownership throughout the KPI lifecycle;
- periodic review of assigned responsibilities.
# 11. KPI Validation and Quality Controls

KPI validation ensures that every reported measurement is reliable, explainable, traceable, and supported by sufficient evidence before being used for governance or decision-making.

## 11.1 Validation Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Verify that measurements correctly represent observed conditions. |
| Completeness | Confirm that all required data and evidence are available. |
| Consistency | Ensure uniform application of approved measurement methodologies. |
| Traceability | Verify linkage between KPI values, calculations, and supporting evidence. |
| Explainability | Ensure results can be independently understood and justified. |
| Auditability | Ensure sufficient documentation exists for independent review. |

---

## 11.2 Quality Control Categories

| Control Category | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Definition Control | Verify that the KPI specification is complete and approved. |
| Data Quality Control | Verify integrity, completeness, and suitability of measurement inputs. |
| Methodology Control | Verify compliance with the approved measurement methodology. |
| Calculation Control | Verify correct application of formulas, normalization, weighting, and aggregation where applicable. |
| Evidence Control | Verify that supporting evidence satisfies established validation requirements. |
| Documentation Control | Verify that all required records are complete and traceable. |

---

## 11.3 Validation Outcomes

Following validation, each KPI measurement shall be assigned one of the following statuses:

| Status | Description |
|---|---|
| Validated | Measurement satisfies all validation requirements. |
| Conditionally Validated | Minor issues identified that do not materially affect interpretation. |
| Validation Required | Additional verification is necessary before use. |
| Rejected | Measurement does not satisfy minimum validation requirements and shall not be used for decision-making. |

Validation activities shall be documented and retained in accordance with the applicable evidence management and governance processes.

# 15. Framework Summary

The KPI Measurement Framework establishes the enterprise methodology governing the complete lifecycle of Key Performance Indicators used within Cognitive Logic assessment services.

By defining standardized principles for KPI governance, design, measurement, validation, reporting, review, and continuous improvement, the framework ensures that every KPI remains measurable, explainable, traceable, auditable, evidence-based, and technology-independent.

The framework supports consistent decision-making across AI Governance, Knowledge Governance, AI Act Readiness, Explainability, Risk, Enterprise, and Organizational Assessments while remaining fully aligned with the Enterprise Delivery Framework and the Sovereign Intelligence Principle defined by ADR-CLE-004.

## 8.7 Baseline Management

A baseline establishes the approved reference condition against which KPI performance, change, and progress are evaluated.

| Baseline Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Reference Period | The time period represented by the baseline shall be explicitly defined. |
| Scope | The organizational, process, assessment, or activity boundary shall be documented. |
| Source Inputs | All data and evidence used to establish the baseline shall be identified. |
| Calculation Method | The baseline shall use the same approved methodology applied to subsequent measurements. |
| Validation Status | The baseline shall be validated before operational use. |
| Effective Date | The date from which the baseline becomes authoritative shall be recorded. |
| Owner | Accountability for baseline maintenance shall be assigned. |
| Version | Every baseline modification shall create a controlled version. |

A baseline shall not be retrospectively altered solely to improve apparent performance.

Baseline recalculation may occur only when:

- the KPI definition materially changes;
- the measurement boundary changes;
- significant data quality issues are identified;
- organizational restructuring invalidates the original reference;
- a governance authority approves the recalculation.

Historical baseline values shall remain preserved for auditability and comparison.

---

## 8.8 Target Values

Target values define the expected or desired level of performance for a KPI within an approved period.

| Target Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| Target Type | Minimum, expected, stretch, maximum, regulatory, or risk-based target. |
| Target Value | Approved quantitative or qualitative result to be achieved. |
| Target Period | Period within which the target is expected to be reached. |
| Rationale | Documented justification supporting the selected target. |
| Approval Authority | Role or governance body responsible for approval. |
| Review Date | Date on which continued target suitability shall be assessed. |

Targets shall be:

- realistic but sufficiently challenging;
- aligned with documented governance objectives;
- measurable using the approved KPI methodology;
- supported by evidence, obligations, benchmarks, or organizational priorities;
- approved before being used for formal evaluation.

---

## 8.9 Thresholds and Tolerances

Thresholds establish performance boundaries. Tolerances define the acceptable deviation around a target, baseline, or expected value.

| Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Lower Threshold | Defines the minimum acceptable result where higher values represent better performance. |
| Upper Threshold | Defines the maximum acceptable result where lower values represent better performance. |
| Warning Threshold | Identifies emerging performance degradation before a critical breach. |
| Critical Threshold | Identifies performance requiring escalation or corrective action. |
| Positive Tolerance | Maximum acceptable deviation above a reference value. |
| Negative Tolerance | Maximum acceptable deviation below a reference value. |

Thresholds and tolerances shall:

- be documented before measurement;
- be based on governance, risk, compliance, operational, or performance requirements;
- avoid retrospective adjustment based on observed results;
- include escalation rules where breaches are material;
- be reviewed whenever objectives, risks, or operating conditions materially change.

## 8.10 Measurement Units and Scales

Every KPI shall use a formally defined measurement unit or assessment scale.

| Unit or Scale | Typical Application |
|---|---|
| Count | Number of occurrences, controls, events, findings, or artefacts. |
| Percentage | Proportion of compliant, complete, available, or achieved elements. |
| Ratio | Relationship between two measured quantities. |
| Duration | Time required to complete an activity or achieve an outcome. |
| Frequency | Number of occurrences within a defined period. |
| Monetary Value | Financial impact, cost, benefit, exposure, or avoided loss. |
| Index | Composite result derived from multiple controlled measurements. |
| Ordinal Scale | Ordered qualitative categories with explicitly defined criteria. |
| Binary Scale | Confirmed or not confirmed, present or absent, satisfied or not satisfied. |

Measurement units shall not be changed during an assessment period without documented impact analysis and governance approval.

---

## 8.11 Missing Data and Measurement Exceptions

Measurement activities shall explicitly identify and manage missing, incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable information.

| Situation | Required Action |
|---|---|
| Missing Data | Document the missing information and assess its impact on KPI reliability. |
| Incomplete Evidence | Suspend or qualify the measurement until validation requirements are satisfied. |
| Inconsistent Measurements | Perform methodological review before reporting results. |
| Exceptional Conditions | Document assumptions, limitations, and governance approvals where deviations from the standard methodology are necessary. |

Estimated or substituted values shall only be used when explicitly authorized by the applicable assessment methodology and shall always be clearly identified within the measurement documentation.

Every measurement exception shall preserve:

- documented justification;
- decision traceability;
- reviewer identification;
- approval history where required;
- impact assessment on KPI interpretation.
