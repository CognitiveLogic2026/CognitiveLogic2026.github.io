# Executive Summary

The Evidence Collection Framework defines the enterprise methodology governing the collection, management, validation, protection, and traceability of evidence throughout Cognitive Logic assessment activities.

The framework establishes a governance-first and evidence-first approach to ensure that every assessment is supported by objective, explainable, auditable, and verifiable evidence.

The framework is technology-independent, provider-independent, and fully aligned with ADR-CLE-004, ensuring that every conclusion is derived from documented evidence rather than assumptions or opaque reasoning.

It applies consistently across all Cognitive Logic assessment services and provides a standardized methodology for evidence acquisition, classification, quality assurance, integrity, traceability, lifecycle management, and continuous improvement.
---

# Scope

This framework governs the complete evidence collection process adopted during Cognitive Logic enterprise assessments.

It defines the principles, governance model, responsibilities, lifecycle, quality criteria, validation requirements, and documentation standards applicable to every evidence collected during assessment activities.

The framework is independent from any technology, software platform, vendor, AI model, cloud provider, or implementation methodology.

---

# Objectives

The framework aims to:

- establish a standardized evidence collection methodology;
- ensure evidence consistency across all assessments;
- guarantee explainability of every assessment outcome;
- maximize evidence quality and reliability;
- support objective decision-making;
- enable complete traceability;
- facilitate independent audits;
- reduce assessment uncertainty;
- improve evidence governance maturity.

---

# Applicability

This framework applies to:

- AI Governance Assessments;
- Knowledge Governance Assessments;
- AI Act Assessments;
- Explainability Assessments;
- Risk Assessments;

# Evidence Governance Model

## Governance Objectives

The Evidence Governance Model establishes the enterprise governance structure responsible for ensuring that evidence collection activities are consistent, objective, controlled, traceable, and auditable throughout the assessment lifecycle.

The governance model defines decision authorities, responsibilities, approval mechanisms, quality controls, and oversight activities governing all evidence-related processes.

## Governance Responsibilities

| Governance Area | Primary Objective |
|---|---|
| Evidence Governance | Define enterprise evidence policies and standards. |
| Evidence Collection | Ensure systematic and consistent acquisition of evidence. |
| Evidence Validation | Verify quality, authenticity, and completeness. |
| Evidence Repository Governance | Govern evidence storage and organization. |
| Evidence Security | Protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability. |
| Evidence Traceability | Maintain complete traceability from source to assessment outcome. |
| Evidence Quality Assurance | Verify compliance with approved quality standards. |
| Continuous Improvement | Continuously improve evidence collection practices. |

## Governance Principles

Evidence governance shall ensure that:

- every evidence item has an identified owner;
- every evidence source is documented;
- every evidence modification is traceable;
- evidence quality is continuously evaluated;
- evidence acceptance follows predefined criteria;
- evidence rejection is documented and justified;
- evidence risks are identified and managed;
- governance decisions remain transparent, explainable, and auditable.
---

# Evidence Lifecycle

The Evidence Lifecycle defines the standard sequence of activities governing every evidence item from its identification to its final disposition.

The lifecycle ensures consistency, integrity, traceability, and governance across all Cognitive Logic assessment services.

| Phase | Objective |
|---|---|
| Planning | Define evidence requirements according to assessment scope and objectives. |
| Identification | Identify relevant evidence sources and expected evidence types. |
| Acquisition | Collect evidence using approved and documented methodologies. |
| Registration | Assign identifiers, metadata, ownership, and classification. |
| Validation | Verify authenticity, integrity, completeness, and reliability. |
| Correlation | Relate evidence to other evidence, findings, risks, and assessment objectives. |
| Utilization | Use validated evidence to support analyses, findings, and recommendations. |
| Review | Periodically review evidence relevance and quality during the assessment. |
| Retention | Preserve evidence according to approved retention policies. |
| Archival or Disposal | Archive or securely dispose of evidence following governance requirements. |

## Lifecycle Requirements

Every lifecycle phase shall:

- be documented;
- be traceable;
- identify responsible roles;
- preserve evidence integrity;
- support independent auditability;
- comply with approved governance policies.

# Evidence Sources

Evidence shall be collected exclusively from identifiable, verifiable, and documented sources appropriate to the assessment scope.

Evidence sources shall always be recorded to ensure transparency, traceability, and auditability.

## Evidence Source Categories

| Source Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Organizational Documentation | Policies, standards, procedures, manuals, and governance documentation. |
| Management Documentation | Strategies, plans, roadmaps, reports, and executive documentation. |
| Operational Documentation | Process documentation, operating procedures, workflows, and work instructions. |
| Interviews | Structured interviews conducted using approved assessment methodologies. |
| Workshops | Facilitated workshops producing documented outputs and shared findings. |
| Direct Observation | Evidence obtained through observation of organizational activities and practices. |
| Organizational Records | Official records supporting governance and operational activities. |
| Assessment Artefacts | Deliverables, working papers, assessment notes, and evidence generated during the assessment itself. |
| Quantitative Indicators | Metrics, measurements, KPIs, and objectively measurable information. |
| External References | Regulations, standards, contractual obligations, and other formally recognized external sources. |

## Source Acceptance Criteria

Every evidence source shall be:

- identifiable;
- accessible to authorized assessors;
- relevant to the assessment objectives;
- sufficiently reliable;
- adequately documented;
- legally and organizationally permissible for assessment purposes.

## Source Documentation Requirements

For each evidence source, the assessment documentation shall record:

| Metadata | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Source Identifier | Unique reference for traceability. |
| Source Description | Brief description of the evidence source. |
| Source Owner | Organizational owner or responsible party. |
| Collection Method | Method used to acquire the evidence. |
| Collection Date | Date of evidence acquisition. |
| Assessor | Individual responsible for collecting the evidence. |
| Confidentiality Level | Required handling and protection level. |
| Related Assessment Area | Assessment domain supported by the evidence. |

# Evidence Acquisition Framework

Evidence acquisition shall follow a standardized, documented, and repeatable methodology to ensure consistency across all Cognitive Logic assessment services.

The acquisition process shall collect sufficient, relevant, objective, and verifiable evidence to support assessment findings and recommendations.

## Evidence Acquisition Process

| Step | Objective |
|---|---|
| Planning | Define evidence requirements, priorities, and expected sources. |
| Preparation | Prepare collection activities, stakeholders, and supporting documentation. |
| Collection | Acquire evidence using approved collection methods. |
| Recording | Register evidence together with mandatory metadata. |
| Initial Verification | Perform a preliminary review of completeness, relevance, and usability. |
| Classification | Apply the approved evidence classification model. |
| Secure Handling | Protect evidence according to confidentiality and security requirements. |
| Submission | Make evidence available for validation and assessment activities. |

## Approved Acquisition Methods

| Method | Typical Use |
|---|---|
| Document Review | Collection of documented organizational information. |
| Structured Interview | Acquisition of information from identified stakeholders. |
| Facilitated Workshop | Collaborative collection and validation of evidence. |
| Direct Observation | Verification of organizational practices and operational activities. |
| Record Examination | Review of official organizational records and documented outputs. |
| Analytical Review | Collection of measurable indicators and analytical information. |

## Acquisition Requirements

Evidence acquisition activities shall:

- follow approved assessment procedures;
- avoid introducing interpretation during collection;
- preserve the original meaning of collected evidence;
- minimize collection bias;
- document any acquisition limitations;
- maintain complete traceability of acquisition activities;
- comply with confidentiality and security requirements.

# Evidence Quality Framework

Evidence quality shall be systematically evaluated before evidence is accepted for assessment purposes.

Quality evaluation ensures that assessment conclusions are supported by objective, reliable, complete, and verifiable evidence.

## Evidence Quality Dimensions

| Quality Dimension | Objective |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Evidence directly supports the assessment objective. |
| Completeness | Evidence provides sufficient information for the intended purpose. |
| Accuracy | Evidence correctly represents the observed facts. |
| Authenticity | Evidence originates from a verifiable and legitimate source. |
| Integrity | Evidence has not been altered or compromised. |
| Reliability | Evidence can be trusted to support assessment decisions. |
| Consistency | Evidence does not materially conflict with validated evidence. |
| Timeliness | Evidence is sufficiently current for the assessment scope. |
| Traceability | Evidence can be traced to its original source and collection process. |
| Verifiability | Independent reviewers can verify the evidence. |

## Evidence Confidence Levels

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Very High | Evidence is independently verifiable, complete, authentic, and strongly corroborated. |
| High | Evidence is reliable with only minor limitations. |
| Moderate | Evidence supports assessment activities but requires additional confirmation. |
| Low | Evidence presents significant limitations and should be corroborated before use. |
| Insufficient | Evidence shall not be used to support assessment conclusions. |

## Evidence Acceptance Criteria

Evidence should normally be accepted when it:

- is relevant to the assessment scope;
- originates from an identifiable source;
- satisfies required quality dimensions;
- is sufficiently complete;
- can be independently verified;
- complies with governance and confidentiality requirements.

## Evidence Rejection Criteria

Evidence should be rejected or escalated for further review when it:

- cannot be traced to a documented source;
- lacks authenticity;
- presents material integrity concerns;
- is incomplete for the intended purpose;
- materially conflicts with validated evidence without documented justification;
- cannot achieve the minimum confidence required by the assessment methodology.

# Evidence Traceability and Chain of Custody

Complete traceability shall be maintained for every evidence item from its initial identification through its final disposition.

The chain of custody shall preserve evidence integrity, accountability, and auditability throughout the assessment lifecycle.

## Traceability Objectives

The traceability model shall ensure that every evidence item can be linked to:

- its original source;
- the collection activity;
- the responsible assessor;
- associated metadata;
- validation activities;
- related findings;
- supported conclusions;
- generated recommendations;
- applicable governance decisions.

## Chain of Custody Requirements

| Control | Objective |
|---|---|
| Unique Identification | Assign a unique identifier to every evidence item. |
| Ownership | Record the organizational owner and responsible assessor. |
| Collection Record | Document when, where, and how evidence was collected. |
| Handling History | Maintain a record of all authorized handling activities. |
| Version History | Record approved changes when evidence is updated. |
| Access History | Record authorized access where required by governance policies. |
| Validation Record | Document validation outcomes and approval decisions. |
| Final Disposition | Record archival or disposal activities. |

## Traceability Matrix

| Traceability Element | Linked To |
|---|---|
| Evidence | Source |
| Evidence | Metadata |
| Evidence | Assessment Area |
| Evidence | Risk |
| Evidence | Finding |
| Evidence | Recommendation |
| Evidence | Validation Record |
| Evidence | Repository Reference |

## Chain of Custody Principles

The chain of custody shall:

- preserve evidence authenticity;
- protect evidence integrity;
- maintain accountability;
- prevent unauthorized modification;
- ensure complete lifecycle traceability;
- support independent audits and governance reviews.

# Evidence Security, Confidentiality and Retention

Evidence shall be protected throughout its lifecycle to preserve confidentiality, integrity, availability, and compliance with applicable governance policies.

Protection measures shall be proportional to the classification and sensitivity of the evidence.

## Confidentiality Levels

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Public | Information approved for unrestricted disclosure. |
| Internal | Information intended exclusively for internal organizational use. |
| Confidential | Information requiring controlled access due to business sensitivity. |
| Restricted | Highly sensitive information requiring enhanced protection and limited authorization. |

## Evidence Security Objectives

| Security Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| Confidentiality | Prevent unauthorized disclosure of evidence. |
| Integrity | Protect evidence from unauthorized alteration or destruction. |
| Availability | Ensure authorized assessors can access evidence when required. |
| Accountability | Maintain records of responsibilities and authorized activities. |
| Protection | Apply appropriate safeguards throughout the evidence lifecycle. |

## Evidence Retention Principles

Evidence shall be retained:

- according to approved organizational policies;
- for the period required by the applicable assessment methodology;
- to support auditability and future reviews;
- while preserving integrity and traceability;
- until authorized archival or disposal activities are completed.

## Evidence Disposal Requirements

Evidence disposal shall:

- follow approved governance procedures;
- be formally authorized;
- preserve confidentiality;
- be documented;
- be traceable;
- ensure that disposal does not compromise regulatory, contractual, or organizational obligations.
---

# Evidence Validation, Acceptance and Gap Management

Evidence shall undergo formal validation before being used to support assessment findings, conclusions, or recommendations.

Validation activities ensure that evidence satisfies the required quality, governance, and traceability standards.

## Validation Prerequisites

Evidence shall satisfy the following prerequisites before validation:

| Prerequisite | Objective |
|---|---|
| Source Identified | The origin of the evidence is documented. |
| Metadata Completed | Mandatory metadata has been recorded. |
| Classification Assigned | Evidence classification has been completed. |
| Integrity Verified | No integrity concerns have been identified. |
| Authenticity Confirmed | The evidence originates from a verifiable source. |
| Traceability Established | Complete traceability has been documented. |
| Quality Reviewed | Evidence quality has been evaluated. |

## Evidence Validation Outcomes

| Outcome | Description |
|---|---|
| Accepted | Evidence fully satisfies validation requirements. |
| Accepted with Limitations | Evidence may be used with documented limitations. |
| Pending | Additional validation activities are required. |
| Rejected | Evidence does not satisfy minimum validation requirements. |

## Evidence Gap Management

Evidence gaps shall be identified whenever available evidence is insufficient to support assessment objectives.

For every identified gap, the assessment team shall document:

- missing evidence;
- affected assessment area;
- associated risks;
- potential impact

# Quality Assurance

Quality assurance activities shall verify that evidence collection processes are consistently executed in accordance with approved enterprise methodologies.

Quality assurance reviews should evaluate:

- compliance with governance requirements;
- consistency of evidence collection practices;
- completeness of documentation;
- adherence to traceability requirements;
- quality of evidence metadata;
- effectiveness of validation activities;
- management of identified evidence gaps;
- implementation of corrective actions.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The Evidence Collection Framework shall be periodically reviewed to improve effectiveness, consistency, and governance maturity.

Continuous improvement activities should include:

- analysis of assessment lessons learned;
- review of recurring evidence quality issues;
- refinement of evidence classification criteria;
- improvement of validation procedures;
- optimization of documentation standards;
- enhancement of governance controls;
- update of KPIs and quality metrics where appropriate.

---

# Compliance

This framework shall be applied in conjunction with the approved Enterprise Delivery Framework documentation.

The framework is fully aligned with ADR-CLE-004 and supports an evidence-first, explainable, auditable, traceable, and technology-independent assessment methodology for all Cognitive Logic enterprise services.
