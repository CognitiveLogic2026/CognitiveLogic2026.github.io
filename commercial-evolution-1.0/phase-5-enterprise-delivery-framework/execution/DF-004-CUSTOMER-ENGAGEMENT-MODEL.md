## 1. Purpose

The Customer Engagement Model defines the principles, structures, responsibilities, interactions, decision points and quality controls governing customer involvement throughout the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Lifecycle.

The model establishes a consistent enterprise-grade approach for engaging customer stakeholders from initial contact through discovery, assessment, analysis, recommendation design, executive review, implementation planning and delivery closure.

Its purpose is to ensure that customer engagement remains:

- structured;
- transparent;
- explainable;
- traceable;
- evidence based;
- decision oriented;
- proportionate to delivery complexity;
- aligned with customer governance;
- measurable through defined indicators;
- independent from specific technologies or providers.

The model transforms customer participation from an informal communication activity into a governed delivery capability.

Customer interactions must contribute directly to one or more of the following outcomes:

- clarification of business objectives;
- validation of delivery scope;
- identification of stakeholders and decision authorities;
- collection and validation of evidence;
- resolution of assumptions and uncertainties;
- alignment on risks and priorities;
- approval of decisions and recommendations;
- preparation of implementation activities;
- confirmation of delivery acceptance and closure.

Customer engagement must not be treated as a parallel administrative process.

It is an integral part of the delivery architecture and must remain connected to:

- governance controls;
- evidence management;
- lifecycle quality gates;
- decision traceability;
- risk and issue management;
- change control;
- executive accountability;
- continuous improvement.

## 2. Scope

This document applies to all Cognitive Logic enterprise services and delivery engagements governed by the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

It covers customer engagement across:

- pre-engagement qualification;
- engagement initiation;
- customer onboarding;
- stakeholder identification;
- executive alignment;
- discovery activities;
- interviews;
- workshops;
- evidence collection;
- assessment execution;
- analysis validation;
- recommendation development;
- governance meetings;
- escalation management;
- executive review;
- implementation planning;
- delivery closure;
- post-engagement feedback.

The model applies to interactions involving:

- executive sponsors;
- business owners;
- service owners;
- process owners;
- governance representatives;
- legal and compliance representatives;
- data and knowledge owners;
- technology representatives;
- risk and audit functions;
- operational teams;
- subject matter experts;
- external partners where formally included in scope.

The model governs both synchronous and asynchronous engagement channels, including:

- meetings;
- workshops;
- interviews;
- structured questionnaires;
- document exchanges;
- evidence requests;
- written approvals;
- governance reviews;
- escalation communications;
- executive presentations;
- delivery acceptance interactions.

The model does not prescribe a specific communication platform, collaboration suite, meeting technology or customer relationship management system.

All supporting technologies must remain subordinate to the approved governance, evidence and decision requirements defined by this framework.

## 4. Customer Engagement Principles

Customer engagement throughout the Enterprise Delivery Lifecycle shall follow the following principles.

### 4.1 Governance First

Customer interactions shall always reinforce governance objectives before operational execution.

### 4.2 Evidence Before Opinion

Statements, assumptions and expectations shall be validated through objective evidence whenever possible.

### 4.3 Transparency

Objectives, responsibilities, decisions and expected outcomes shall remain visible to all authorised stakeholders.

### 4.4 Explainability

Every significant recommendation or decision shall be explainable using documented evidence and explicit reasoning.

### 4.5 Traceability

All relevant engagement activities shall be traceable across the delivery lifecycle.

### 4.6 Executive Alignment

Strategic decisions shall remain aligned with executive objectives and approved governance priorities.

### 4.7 Shared Accountability

Delivery success depends upon clearly defined responsibilities shared between Cognitive Logic and the customer organisation.

### 4.8 Continuous Communication

Communication shall remain structured, timely, consistent and proportionate to delivery complexity.

### 4.9 Respect for Organisational Context

Engagement activities shall adapt to the customer's governance structure without compromising framework integrity.

### 4.10 Continuous Improvement

Lessons learned from customer interactions shall contribute to improving future delivery engagements.
## 5. Stakeholder Identification

Effective customer engagement begins with the identification of all stakeholders who influence, support, approve or are impacted by the delivery.

Stakeholder identification shall be completed during the engagement initiation phase and maintained throughout the lifecycle.

The objective is to ensure:

- complete governance visibility;
- appropriate decision authority;
- balanced representation of business and technical perspectives;
- effective communication;
- accountable decision making.

### 5.1 Stakeholder Categories

Typical stakeholder categories include:

| Stakeholder Category | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive Sponsor | Strategic direction and executive approvals |
| Business Owner | Business objectives and expected outcomes |
| Service Owner | Operational ownership of the service |
| Process Owner | Business process validation |
| Governance Representative | Governance oversight |
| Compliance Representative | Regulatory and policy alignment |
| Risk Representative | Risk identification and validation |
| Technology Representative | Technical feasibility and integration |
| Knowledge Owner | Validation of organisational knowledge |
| Subject Matter Expert | Domain expertise |
| Project Coordinator | Operational coordination |
| Delivery Lead | Delivery governance and execution |

### 5.2 Stakeholder Mapping

Stakeholders should be mapped according to:

- authority;
- accountability;
- influence;
- decision rights;
- required participation;
- communication frequency;
- expected deliverables.

The stakeholder map shall be reviewed whenever organisational changes materially affect the delivery.

## 6. Customer Roles and Responsibilities

Customer responsibilities shall be explicitly agreed before execution activities begin.

Typical customer responsibilities include:

- providing access to relevant stakeholders;
- supplying agreed evidence;
- validating factual information;
- participating in interviews and workshops;
- reviewing intermediate findings;
- approving agreed decision points;
- escalating organisational blockers when required;
- supporting implementation planning;
- participating in delivery closure activities.

Responsibilities shall be proportionate to the agreed scope and documented as part of the engagement governance.
## 7. Communication Model

The communication model establishes structured interactions between Cognitive Logic and the customer throughout the delivery lifecycle.

Communication shall be:

- planned;
- objective;
- timely;
- evidence based;
- proportional to delivery complexity;
- aligned with governance responsibilities.

### 7.1 Communication Objectives

The communication model supports:

- executive visibility;
- operational coordination;
- decision enablement;
- issue resolution;
- risk communication;
- evidence validation;
- expectation management.

### 7.2 Communication Channels

Typical communication channels include:

| Communication Activity | Typical Participants | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Alignment Meeting | Executive Sponsor, Delivery Lead | At major milestones |
| Governance Review | Governance stakeholders | As required by lifecycle |
| Discovery Session | Business stakeholders | Discovery phase |
| Workshop | Cross-functional stakeholders | Planned sessions |
| Interview | Individual stakeholders | Scheduled |
| Evidence Review | Customer and delivery team | During assessment |
| Decision Review | Decision authorities | At decision gates |
| Delivery Closure Meeting | Executive Sponsor and Delivery Lead | End of engagement |

### 7.3 Communication Records

Significant communications shall be documented whenever they influence:

- scope;
- priorities;
- governance;
- evidence;
- decisions;
- risks;
- recommendations;
- implementation planning.

## 8. Discovery Engagement

Discovery represents the first structured interaction phase of the delivery lifecycle.

Its objectives include:

- understanding business objectives;
- identifying organisational context;
- defining governance expectations;
- identifying stakeholders;
- confirming delivery scope;
- identifying available evidence;
- establishing engagement priorities;
- recognising assumptions and constraints.

Discovery activities shall conclude with a shared understanding of the engagement baseline before assessment execution begins.
## 9. Executive Alignment

Executive alignment ensures that strategic objectives remain consistent throughout the delivery lifecycle.

Executive engagement shall focus on:

- business priorities;
- governance objectives;
- organisational constraints;
- expected business outcomes;
- strategic risks;
- decision authority;
- delivery expectations.

Executive alignment meetings should occur at key lifecycle milestones to confirm continued sponsorship and strategic direction.

## 10. Governance Meetings

Governance meetings provide formal oversight of delivery execution.

Typical governance meeting objectives include:

- reviewing delivery progress;
- validating evidence completeness;
- monitoring quality indicators;
- reviewing risks and issues;
- confirming decision readiness;
- approving lifecycle transitions;
- identifying corrective actions.

Meeting outcomes shall be documented and linked to the relevant governance artefacts.

## 11. Workshop Integration

Workshops enable collaborative exploration of business processes, governance requirements and organisational knowledge.

Workshop activities may support:

- process analysis;
- governance design;
- knowledge discovery;
- maturity assessment;
- recommendation validation;
- implementation planning.

Workshop outputs shall be consolidated into documented evidence.

## 12. Interview Management

Interviews provide structured collection of stakeholder knowledge.

Interview management shall define:

- interview objectives;
- participant selection;
- preparation activities;
- evidence capture;
- validation process;
- confidentiality requirements;
- approval where required.

Interview findings shall remain attributable, traceable and subject to validation before influencing delivery decisions.
## 13. Decision Points

Customer participation is required at defined governance decision points throughout the Enterprise Delivery Lifecycle.

Typical decision points include:

- engagement confirmation;
- scope validation;
- discovery completion;
- evidence sufficiency;
- assessment completion;
- recommendation review;
- executive approval;
- implementation planning;
- delivery acceptance.

Each decision point shall identify:

- required inputs;
- responsible decision authority;
- approval criteria;
- expected outputs;
- associated evidence.

## 14. Escalation Process

Escalation shall be initiated whenever issues threaten delivery objectives, governance integrity or customer expectations.

Typical escalation triggers include:

- unresolved risks;
- conflicting stakeholder decisions;
- missing critical evidence;
- repeated delivery delays;
- governance non-conformance;
- significant scope changes;
- resource constraints.

Escalation activities shall be documented together with:

- issue description;
- impact assessment;
- proposed resolution;
- accountable owner;
- target resolution date;
- final outcome.

## 15. Customer Experience Principles

Customer engagement shall deliver a professional, predictable and transparent experience.

Customer interactions should remain:

- respectful;
- responsive;
- evidence driven;
- solution oriented;
- proportionate;
- measurable;
- continuously improved.

The quality of the customer experience shall never compromise governance, evidence quality or decision integrity.
## 16. Evidence Collection Interaction

Customer participation is essential for effective evidence collection.

Evidence collection interactions shall support:

- objective fact gathering;
- document validation;
- organisational knowledge capture;
- governance verification;
- regulatory assessment;
- KPI measurement;
- recommendation development.

Customer-provided evidence shall be evaluated according to the approved Evidence Framework before being considered authoritative.

## 17. Communication Quality

Communication quality shall be monitored throughout the delivery lifecycle.

Minimum quality characteristics include:

- clarity;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- timeliness;
- traceability;
- appropriateness for the intended audience.

Communication deficiencies shall be analysed as opportunities for continuous improvement.

## 18. KPI

The effectiveness of customer engagement should be measured using defined indicators.

| KPI | Measurement Objective |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder Participation Rate | Planned versus actual participation |
| Executive Alignment Rate | Executive approvals completed on schedule |
| Meeting Effectiveness | Achievement of meeting objectives |
| Evidence Response Time | Timeliness of customer evidence submission |
| Decision Cycle Time | Time required for governance decisions |
| Customer Satisfaction | Perceived quality of engagement |
| Escalation Resolution Time | Time required to resolve escalations |
| Delivery Communication Quality | Conformance to communication standards |

## 19. Continuous Improvement

Lessons learned from each engagement shall be incorporated into future delivery activities.

Continuous improvement activities include:

- customer feedback analysis;
- retrospective reviews;
- communication improvements;
- governance refinements;
- stakeholder engagement optimisation;
- documentation updates;
- quality improvement initiatives.

## 20. Architectural Notes

The Customer Engagement Model is an architectural component of the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

It provides the governance structure through which customer interactions support evidence collection, explainability, traceability and accountable decision making.

The model remains technology independent and shall evolve consistently with future updates to the Enterprise Delivery Framework.
