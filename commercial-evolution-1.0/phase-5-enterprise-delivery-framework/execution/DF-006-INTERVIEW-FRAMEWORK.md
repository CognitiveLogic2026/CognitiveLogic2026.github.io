## 1. Purpose

The Interview Framework defines the enterprise methodology governing the planning, preparation, execution, documentation, validation and controlled use of interviews performed throughout Cognitive Logic delivery engagements.

The framework establishes a standardized, repeatable, auditable and evidence-based approach for acquiring organizational knowledge while ensuring that information collected during interviews is appropriately classified, contextualized, validated and integrated into the assessment process.

Interviews are considered structured knowledge acquisition activities rather than evidence generation activities. Their purpose is to collect, clarify and contextualize information that may subsequently contribute to assessments only after appropriate verification and corroboration.

The framework applies the principles defined by ADR-CLE-004 — QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle, ensuring that interview outcomes never replace objective evidence, measurable observations or verified organizational facts.

Accordingly, statements made by stakeholders shall always be treated as informational inputs whose reliability depends on their source, context, corroboration and validation.

No interview, regardless of the authority of the participant, shall independently determine findings, governance conclusions, risk ratings or recommendations without integration with intelligible and verifiable evidence.

## 2. Scope

This framework applies to every Cognitive Logic service involving structured stakeholder interviews, including but not limited to:

- Discovery engagements;
- Enterprise assessments;
- Governance analysis;
- Knowledge discovery initiatives;
- Risk assessments;
- Governance maturity evaluations;
- AI Act readiness assessments;
- Explainability analyses;
- Decision traceability activities;
- Executive advisory engagements;
- Implementation planning;
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

The framework is service-independent and shall be adopted consistently across all enterprise delivery activities requiring structured information gathering from organizational stakeholders.

## 4. Interview Principles

Every interview conducted under this framework shall comply with the following principles.

### 4.1 Evidence Before Conclusion

Interview outputs shall never be interpreted as validated evidence without an appropriate verification process.

### 4.2 Neutrality

Interviewers shall maintain professional neutrality throughout the engagement, avoiding influence over stakeholder responses.

### 4.3 Traceability

Every relevant statement shall remain attributable to its origin, context and interview session.

### 4.4 Context Preservation

Statements shall never be interpreted outside the organizational, procedural and temporal context in which they were expressed.

### 4.5 Explainability

Any assessment input derived from interviews shall remain explainable, including the reasoning used to classify, validate and integrate collected information.

### 4.6 Proportional Reliability

The confidence assigned to interview information shall be proportional to:

- stakeholder competence;
- organizational authority;
- supporting evidence;
- corroboration by additional sources;
- consistency with observable facts.

### 4.7 Independence

Interviews shall support—not replace—document analysis, evidence collection, governance reviews, technical verification and objective assessment activities.

### 4.8 Confidentiality

Information shall be managed according to the agreed confidentiality level, applicable regulations and contractual obligations.

## 5. Interview Governance

Interview activities constitute governed delivery processes and shall therefore be planned, executed and reviewed according to defined governance controls.

Governance responsibilities include:

- interview authorization;
- stakeholder identification;
- interview planning;
- protocol compliance;
- quality assurance;
- evidence integration;
- information protection;
- escalation management;
- audit readiness;
- continuous improvement.

No interview may be considered complete until all mandatory governance controls have been satisfied and the resulting information has entered the defined validation workflow.
## 6. Interview Lifecycle

Every interview shall follow a standardized lifecycle ensuring consistency, repeatability and governance throughout the engagement.

| Phase | Primary Objective | Main Output |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Define purpose, scope and stakeholders | Interview Plan |
| Preparation | Prepare protocol, questions and logistics | Interview Package |
| Execution | Conduct structured interview | Interview Notes |
| Documentation | Produce structured record | Interview Record |
| Validation | Review accuracy and completeness | Validated Interview Summary |
| Corroboration | Compare with additional sources | Corroboration Assessment |
| Integration | Classify assessment inputs | Assessment Input Register |
| Closure | Archive and monitor actions | Closed Interview Record |

Each lifecycle phase includes mandatory quality controls before progression to the next phase.

## 7. Interview Planning

Interview planning establishes the foundation for an effective knowledge acquisition activity.

Planning shall define at minimum:

- interview objective;
- expected assessment contribution;
- service context;
- organizational scope;
- stakeholders involved;
- interview type;
- required competencies;
- estimated duration;
- confidentiality level;
- expected outputs;
- dependencies;
- risks;
- required evidence to be reviewed before the interview.

The Interview Lead shall ensure that interviews are scheduled only after sufficient background information has been collected to enable meaningful discussion.

## 8. Interview Preparation

Preparation activities shall ensure that interviewers possess sufficient organizational and assessment context before engaging stakeholders.

Preparation shall include:

- review of available documentation;
- review of governance artefacts;
- review of organizational structure;
- review of previous assessments where applicable;
- review of identified risks;
- review of known evidence gaps;
- identification of validation objectives;
- preparation of interview guide;
- preparation of note-taking template;
- confirmation of logistics.

Preparation shall minimize unnecessary questions and maximize the value of stakeholder time while improving consistency across interview sessions.

## 9. Interview Types

Interview type shall be selected according to the objective of the assessment rather than organizational hierarchy.

Typical interview categories include:

- Executive Interview;
- Sponsor Interview;
- Business Owner Interview;
- Process Owner Interview;
- Governance Interview;
- Compliance Interview;
- Knowledge Owner Interview;
- Technical Interview;
- Operational Interview;
- Risk Interview;
- Validation Interview;
- Follow-up Interview;
- Exit Interview;
- Lessons Learned Interview.

Each interview type shall follow the common governance model defined by this framework while applying objectives, techniques and outputs appropriate to its specific purpose.

## 11. Stakeholder Selection

Stakeholder selection shall be risk-based, objective-driven and proportional to the scope of the engagement.

Selection shall consider at minimum:

- organizational role;
- decision authority;
- domain expertise;
- ownership of relevant processes;
- knowledge availability;
- operational responsibilities;
- governance responsibilities;
- involvement in assessed activities;
- independence where required;
- potential conflicts of interest.

Stakeholder selection shall ensure balanced organizational representation and shall avoid relying on a single source of information for critical assessment areas.

Where significant knowledge gaps remain, additional stakeholders shall be identified through iterative planning.

### 12.2 Segregation of Duties

Where appropriate, interview execution shall separate the following responsibilities:

- interview facilitation;
- documentation;
- evidence validation;
- quality review;
- assessment decision-making.

The same individual should not simultaneously conduct the interview, validate disputed evidence and approve resulting assessment conclusions unless explicitly justified and documented.

## 13. Interview Design

Interview design shall ensure that every interview remains aligned with the assessment objective.

The design process shall define:

- interview objective;
- expected information domains;
- required stakeholder profile;
- interview duration;
- interview format;
- evidence objectives;
- validation objectives;
- expected outputs;
- confidentiality requirements;
- follow-up criteria.

Interview guides shall be sufficiently structured to ensure consistency while allowing flexibility for evidence-driven exploration when justified.
## 14. Question Design

Question design shall maximize information quality while minimizing interviewer influence and cognitive bias.

Each question shall have a clearly defined objective and contribute to one or more assessment goals.

### 14.1 Recommended Question Types

| Question Type | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|
| Open Questions | Encourage detailed explanations and context. |
| Closed Questions | Confirm specific facts or conditions. |
| Clarification Questions | Resolve ambiguity or incomplete statements. |
| Probing Questions | Explore underlying causes and rationale. |
| Confirmation Questions | Verify understanding or summarize responses. |
| Comparative Questions | Compare practices across periods, teams or processes. |
| Temporal Questions | Establish chronology and sequence of events. |
| Scenario-Based Questions | Understand expected behaviour under defined situations. |
| Evidence-Based Questions | Request supporting documents or observable artefacts. |
| Governance Questions | Explore accountability, decision-making and oversight. |
| Risk Questions | Identify perceived threats, controls and mitigations. |
| Control Questions | Assess operational and governance controls. |
| Maturity Questions | Evaluate capability development and consistency. |
| Responsibility Questions | Clarify ownership and accountability. |
| Decision Questions | Understand how decisions are made and documented. |
| Traceability Questions | Verify how decisions can be reconstructed. |
| Exception Questions | Identify deviations from standard processes. |
| Real Case Questions | Collect practical examples supporting discussion. |

### 14.2 Restricted Question Types

The following shall be avoided or explicitly controlled:

- leading questions;
- loaded questions;
- ambiguous questions;
- compound or multiple questions;
- coercive questions;
- questions based on unverified assumptions;
- questions requesting opinions outside the participant's competence;
- questions compromising neutrality, confidentiality or professional ethics.

## 15. Interview Protocol

Every interview shall follow a controlled protocol.

1. Confirm participant identity and organizational role.
2. Explain interview objectives.
3. Explain how collected information will be used.
4. Communicate confidentiality rules.
5. Obtain recording consent where applicable.
6. Confirm expected duration.
7. Conduct structured questioning.
8. Request supporting examples and evidence references.
9. Verify mutual understanding throughout the discussion.
10. Summarize key points before closing.
11. Confirm follow-up actions.
12. Close the interview formally.
13. Classify collected information.
14. Register interview outputs.
15. Initiate validation and corroboration activities.

Protocol deviations shall be documented together with their justification and potential impact on assessment quality.
## 16. Interview Conduct

Interview execution shall follow a structured yet adaptive approach that balances consistency with the flexibility required to investigate emerging topics relevant to the assessment.

Interviewers shall:

- maintain professional neutrality;
- follow the approved interview guide;
- adapt sequencing without compromising objectives;
- distinguish facts from interpretations;
- encourage evidence-supported responses;
- document significant observations in real time;
- avoid premature conclusions;
- periodically verify shared understanding.

Unexpected topics may be explored when they contribute to the assessment objective, provided that such deviations are documented.

## 17. Opening and Closing Procedures

### 17.1 Opening Procedure

At the beginning of each interview the Interview Lead shall ensure that participants understand:

- the purpose of the interview;
- the scope of discussion;
- expected duration;
- confidentiality arrangements;
- recording conditions, where applicable;
- intended use of collected information;
- participant rights;
- opportunity to request clarification at any time.

### 17.2 Closing Procedure

Before concluding the interview, the interviewer shall:

- summarize key discussion points;
- distinguish confirmed information from pending validation;
- identify requested supporting evidence;
- confirm outstanding follow-up actions;
- explain subsequent validation activities;
- thank participants for their contribution.

## 18. Facilitation Techniques

Interview facilitation shall encourage accurate, complete and balanced information gathering.

Recommended techniques include:

- progressive questioning;
- structured topic transitions;
- reflective summarization;
- clarification before interpretation;
- controlled silence to encourage elaboration;
- evidence-oriented discussion;
- periodic recap of key statements;
- explicit identification of assumptions requiring validation.

Facilitation techniques shall never manipulate stakeholder responses or intentionally influence conclusions.

## 19. Neutrality and Bias Controls

Interview quality depends upon systematic control of cognitive and organizational bias.

Interviewers shall actively mitigate:

- confirmation bias;
- authority bias;
- anchoring bias;
- availability bias;
- recency bias;
- interviewer expectation bias;
- organizational hierarchy bias;
- groupthink during multi-participant interviews.

Mitigation measures include:

- standardized interview guides;
- evidence-oriented questioning;
- independent corroboration;
- structured note-taking;
- peer review where appropriate;
- comparison across multiple stakeholders;
- documented rationale for assessment interpretations.

## 20. Active Listening and Follow-up Questions

Interviewers shall apply active listening techniques throughout the interview.

These include:

- attentive listening without interruption;
- confirmation of understanding;
- paraphrasing when appropriate;
- clarification of ambiguous terminology;
- identification of inconsistencies;
- separation of observation from interpretation.

Follow-up questions shall be used to:

- clarify incomplete statements;
- understand causal relationships;
- identify supporting evidence;
- verify chronology;
- resolve inconsistencies;
- distinguish assumptions from observable facts;
- improve contextual understanding without introducing interviewer bias.
## 21. Sensitive Topics

Sensitive topics shall be addressed only when necessary to achieve the assessment objective and shall be handled with professionalism, proportionality and respect.

Interviewers shall:

- explain why the topic is relevant;
- avoid unnecessary personal questions;
- remain neutral;
- avoid assumptions;
- allow participants adequate time to respond;
- suspend discussion if confidentiality concerns arise;
- escalate legal or ethical concerns according to the governance model.

Where a participant declines to answer, the refusal shall be documented without pressure or coercion.

## 22. Confidentiality

Information collected during interviews shall be protected according to its classification, contractual obligations and applicable legal requirements.

Interview documentation shall clearly identify:

- confidentiality level;
- permitted audience;
- storage requirements;
- distribution restrictions;
- retention requirements;
- disposal requirements where applicable.

Confidentiality obligations apply equally to interview recordings, notes, summaries and supporting documentation.

## 23. Consent and Transparency

Participants shall receive sufficient information before the interview to understand:

- interview purpose;
- expected duration;
- intended use of collected information;
- documentation approach;
- recording arrangements, where applicable;
- confidentiality protections;
- validation process.

Where recording is planned, explicit consent shall be obtained before recording begins.

Consent status shall be documented within the interview record.

## 24. Recording and Note-Taking

Interview documentation shall distinguish clearly between:

- verbatim quotations;
- summarized statements;
- interviewer observations;
- interpretations;
- identified evidence references;
- follow-up actions.

Interview notes shall remain factual, attributable and sufficiently detailed to support later validation.

If recording is not permitted, structured note-taking becomes mandatory.

## 25. Information Classification

Every significant item collected during an interview shall receive an information classification.

Minimum classifications include:

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| Unverified Statement | Information not yet validated. |
| Stakeholder Perception | Subjective opinion or personal assessment. |
| Contextual Information | Background information supporting understanding. |
| Asserted Fact | Fact declared but not yet verified. |
| Evidence Reference | Reference to an existing document or artefact. |
| Corroborated Statement | Statement confirmed by independent sources. |
| Validated Fact | Information successfully verified. |
| Assessment Finding Input | Validated information eligible for assessment activities. |

Classification shall be reviewed whenever new corroborating evidence becomes available.

## 26. Interview Evidence Integration

Interview outputs shall be integrated into the assessment only through the governed evidence process.

Interview information shall never bypass:

- evidence collection;
- evidence validation;
- corroboration;
- quality review;
- assessment governance.

Statements obtained during interviews shall support assessment activities only after appropriate validation consistent with:

- DF-005 — Assessment Execution Framework;
- DF-008 — Evidence Collection Framework;
- DF-009 — Evidence Validation Process;
- DF-011 — Recommendation Framework.

No single interview shall independently generate a verified finding, governance conclusion or recommendation.
## 27. Corroboration

Information collected during interviews shall be corroborated whenever it contributes materially to assessment findings, governance conclusions or recommendations.

Corroboration activities may include:

- document review;
- evidence inspection;
- observation of operational activities;
- system and process verification;
- interviews with additional stakeholders;
- comparison with historical records;
- KPI analysis;
- governance documentation review.

Corroboration shall evaluate:

- consistency;
- completeness;
- plausibility;
- traceability;
- source reliability.

## 28. Validation

Interview validation ensures that documented information accurately represents what was communicated during the interview.

Validation activities may include:

- internal quality review;
- participant confirmation where appropriate;
- evidence verification;
- consistency review across interviews;
- assessment team peer review.

Validation shall distinguish clearly between:

- confirmed information;
- corrected information;
- unresolved statements;
- rejected interpretations.

## 29. Contradiction Management

Conflicting stakeholder statements shall be treated as assessment inputs requiring additional investigation rather than immediate resolution.

When contradictions are identified, the assessment team shall:

- document each conflicting statement;
- preserve attribution;
- identify potential causes;
- request supporting evidence;
- perform additional corroboration;
- determine whether escalation is required.

Contradictions shall remain traceable throughout the assessment lifecycle.

## 30. Non-Response Management

Failure or refusal to provide information shall not automatically be interpreted as evidence of absence or organizational deficiency.

Where responses cannot be obtained, the Interview Lead shall document:

- unavailable participant;
- declined response;
- unavailable evidence;
- scheduling constraints;
- organizational restrictions;
- potential assessment impact.

Significant information gaps shall be escalated according to the delivery governance framework.

## 31. Interview Deviations

Any deviation from the approved interview protocol shall be documented.

Examples include:

- reduced interview duration;
- unavailable stakeholders;
- technical interruptions;
- unexpected participants;
- protocol changes;
- confidentiality restrictions;
- incomplete coverage of planned topics.

Each deviation shall include:

- description;
- justification;
- impact assessment;
- mitigation actions;
- approval where required.

## 32. Quality Controls

Interview quality shall be monitored throughout the engagement.

Minimum quality controls include:

- interview plan approval;
- interview guide review;
- protocol compliance verification;
- documentation completeness review;
- evidence traceability verification;
- classification review;
- corroboration review;
- quality assurance review;
- audit readiness verification.

Quality Gates shall prevent interview outputs from progressing into assessment findings until mandatory controls have been completed.

## 33. Risk Management

Interview-related risks shall be identified, monitored and managed.

Typical risks include:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Interviewer bias | Standardized methodology and peer review |
| Authority bias | Multiple stakeholder perspectives |
| Missing stakeholders | Stakeholder coverage review |
| Insufficient evidence | Mandatory corroboration |
| Confidentiality breach | Information classification controls |
| Misinterpretation | Validation and participant clarification |
| Documentation errors | Structured templates and QA review |
| Scope drift | Approved interview objectives |

## 34. Escalation Rules

Escalation shall occur whenever interview outcomes may materially affect assessment integrity.

Typical escalation triggers include:

- significant contradictions;
- unavailable critical stakeholders;
- suspected misinformation;
- evidence manipulation concerns;
- confidentiality incidents;
- ethical concerns;
- unresolved governance issues;
- major assessment risks.

Escalations shall follow the governance model defined by DF-003.

## 35. Interview Outputs

## 37. Continuous Improvement

Lessons learned from interview activities shall be incorporated into future delivery engagements.

Continuous improvement activities include:

- periodic framework reviews;
- interviewer feedback;
- quality trend analysis;
- assessment retrospectives;
- refinement of interview guides;
- updates to governance controls;
- improvement of knowledge acquisition techniques.

Improvement actions shall be documented, prioritized and monitored according to DF-016.
## 38. Architectural Notes

The Interview Framework constitutes a foundational execution component of the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.

Its primary architectural objective is to ensure that organizational knowledge acquired through interviews remains:

- technology independent;
- provider independent;
- evidence based;
- explainable;
- traceable;
- measurable;
- auditable;
- repeatable;
- governance driven.

Interview activities shall never replace evidence collection, objective measurement or governance analysis.

The framework explicitly supports the architectural principles defined by ADR-CLE-004, ensuring that assessment decisions originate from intelligible data, verifiable evidence, the Knowledge Graph, the Governance Engine, the QEN Sovereign Engine and proprietary reasoning algorithms rather than subjective stakeholder opinion alone.

Interview outputs therefore represent governed assessment inputs whose confidence depends upon classification, validation and corroboration.

### Framework Summary

The Interview Framework establishes a standardized enterprise methodology for acquiring organizational knowledge through structured interviews while preventing subjective declarations from being interpreted as verified evidence.

By enforcing governance controls, information classification, validation, corroboration and traceability, the framework ensures that interview-derived information contributes to assessments in a controlled, explainable and auditable manner.

No interview outcome, regardless of stakeholder authority or organizational position, shall independently determine findings, conclusions or recommendations without integration with verifiable evidence and the governed assessment processes defined throughout the Enterprise Delivery Framework.
