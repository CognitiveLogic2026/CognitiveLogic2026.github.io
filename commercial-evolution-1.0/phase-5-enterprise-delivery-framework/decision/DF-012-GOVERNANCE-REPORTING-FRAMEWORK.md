# 1. Purpose

The Governance Reporting Framework defines the enterprise methodology governing the production, management, validation, approval, distribution, traceability and continuous improvement of reporting activities generated throughout Cognitive Logic delivery services.

The framework establishes standardized governance principles to ensure that every report is evidence-based, explainable, traceable, auditable, measurable and fully independent from specific technologies, software platforms or AI providers.

This framework governs reporting processes only.

It does not define report templates, layouts or executive report contents, which are specified separately within the Reporting Standards documentation.
---

# 2. Scope

This framework applies to every reporting activity performed within the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.

Its purpose is to establish a consistent governance model for report generation independently of the service being delivered, the organizational context, the supporting technologies or the execution environment.

The framework applies to reports produced during:

- AI Governance Assessments
- Knowledge Governance Assessments
- AI Act Readiness Assessments
- Explainability Assessments
- Enterprise Risk Assessments
- Organizational Assessments
- Executive Advisory Services
- Delivery Governance Activities
- Evidence Review Processes
- Decision Support Activities

This framework governs the complete reporting lifecycle, including:

- reporting governance;
- report planning;
- information collection;
- evidence integration;
- report production;
- quality assurance;
- validation;
- approval;
- controlled distribution;
- version management;
- traceability;
- explainability;
- auditability;
- retention;
- continuous improvement.

This document defines the governance methodology of reporting.

It does not prescribe report templates, graphical layouts, presentation formats or document structures intended for executive communication, which are addressed by dedicated reporting standards.

# 4. Reporting Governance Principles

Reporting governance is founded on a common set of enterprise principles that ensure consistency, reliability and accountability across all reporting activities.

These principles shall be applied throughout the complete reporting lifecycle and shall govern every report regardless of service domain, assessment methodology, organizational context or supporting technology.

| Principle | Governance Objective | Practical Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Governance First | Reporting shall support governance before communication. | Reports are produced to enable informed governance decisions rather than merely documenting activities. |
| Evidence First | Every conclusion shall be supported by validated evidence. | Unsupported statements, assumptions and opinions shall not be presented as findings. |
| Measurement First | Reported conclusions shall rely on measurable criteria whenever applicable. | Indicators, metrics and documented evaluation methods shall be preferred over subjective judgement. |
| Explainability | Reporting logic shall be understandable by stakeholders. | Findings, recommendations and conclusions shall clearly describe the reasoning process. |
| Traceability | Every reported outcome shall be traceable to its originating evidence and governance decisions. | End-to-end traceability shall be maintained throughout the reporting lifecycle. |
| Auditability | Reporting activities shall be independently verifiable. | Reviews, approvals, revisions and supporting evidence shall remain available for audit purposes. |
| Accountability | Responsibilities shall be explicitly assigned. | Ownership, review and approval authorities shall be clearly identified for every report. |
| Standardization | Reporting practices shall follow common enterprise standards. | Consistent terminology, governance rules and reporting processes shall be applied across all services. |
| Technology Independence | Reporting methodology shall remain independent from implementation technologies. | Governance rules shall not depend on specific software platforms or technical architectures. |
| Provider Independence | Reporting processes shall not rely on proprietary AI providers. | Conclusions shall remain valid regardless of the technologies used to support delivery activities. |
| Information Integrity | Reported information shall remain accurate, complete and consistent. | Quality controls shall preserve the integrity of reporting outputs throughout their lifecycle. |
| Continuous Improvement | Reporting governance shall evolve through measurable improvement activities. | Reviews, lessons learned and quality metrics shall drive progressive refinement of reporting practices. |

These principles operationalize the Governance First, Evidence First and Measurement First approach adopted throughout the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework and ensure full alignment with ADR-CLE-004 — QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle.

# 5. Reporting Lifecycle

The reporting lifecycle defines the standardized governance process through which enterprise reports are planned, produced, validated, approved, distributed and continuously improved.

Each phase establishes governance objectives, expected outcomes and control activities while maintaining complete alignment with the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

| Phase | Primary Objective | Primary Output |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting Planning | Define reporting objectives, scope, stakeholders and governance requirements. | Reporting plan |
| Information Collection | Gather validated information, assessment results and approved inputs. | Reporting dataset |
| Evidence Integration | Associate all reported statements with verified evidence. | Evidence-linked reporting content |
| Report Development | Produce the report according to enterprise reporting standards. | Draft report |
| Quality Review | Verify completeness, consistency, clarity and methodological compliance. | Reviewed draft |
| Validation | Confirm that findings accurately reflect validated evidence and approved decisions. | Validated report |
| Approval | Obtain formal authorization from designated reporting authorities. | Approved report |
| Controlled Distribution | Deliver the approved report to authorized recipients according to governance policies. | Distributed report |
| Lifecycle Maintenance | Manage revisions, version history, retention and continuous improvement. | Maintained reporting record |

The reporting lifecycle is iterative rather than purely sequential. New evidence, governance decisions, quality findings or organizational changes may require controlled revisions while preserving full traceability of every reporting activity.

Each lifecycle phase shall define clear responsibilities, documented inputs, expected outputs and measurable quality controls to ensure reporting remains reliable, explainable, auditable and technology-independent throughout its entire lifecycle.

# 6. Report Taxonomy and Hierarchy

The Governance Reporting Framework classifies reports according to their governance purpose rather than their presentation format.

This taxonomy provides a consistent enterprise model for organizing reporting activities across all Cognitive Logic services while preserving methodological consistency, traceability and auditability.

## 6.1 Report Taxonomy

| Report Category | Governance Purpose | Typical Consumers |
|---|---|---|
| Governance Reports | Support governance oversight and strategic decision-making. | Executive Management, Governance Boards |
| Assessment Reports | Present assessment findings, analyses and conclusions. | Customers, Delivery Teams, Decision Authorities |
| Evidence Reports | Document validated evidence supporting governance activities. | Assessors, Reviewers, Auditors |
| Decision Support Reports | Provide structured information for governance decisions. | Decision Authorities |
| Risk Reports | Describe identified risks, impacts and governance considerations. | Risk Owners, Executive Management |
| Quality Reports | Monitor reporting quality, process compliance and improvement opportunities. | Quality Assurance, Governance Management |
| Audit Reports | Support internal and external audit activities through documented reporting records. | Auditors, Compliance Authorities |

## 6.2 Reporting Hierarchy

Reports shall be organized according to governance authority and decision relevance.

| Reporting Level | Primary Objective | Governance Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Reporting | Support executive governance and organizational direction. | Enterprise |
| Tactical Reporting | Support programme, portfolio and service governance. | Business Unit or Service |
| Operational Reporting | Support delivery execution and operational control. | Engagement or Assessment |
| Supporting Reporting | Provide detailed evidence, appendices and reference information. | Individual Activities or Work Products |

The reporting hierarchy ensures that information is communicated at an appropriate level of abstraction while maintaining full consistency between executive conclusions and supporting evidence.

Every report shall clearly identify its reporting category, governance level and intended audience to ensure controlled production, review, approval and distribution throughout the reporting lifecycle.

A report shall be formally defined before information collection begins to ensure consistency, accountability and alignment with governance objectives.

| Reporting Level | Assign the appropriate governance hierarchy. | Mandatory |
| Information Sources | Identify the approved information inputs. | Mandatory |
| Evidence Requirements | Define the evidence expected to support reported conclusions. | Mandatory |
| Approval Authority | Identify the authority responsible for approving the report. | Mandatory |
| Distribution Rules | Define authorized distribution constraints. | Mandatory |
| Retention Requirements | Specify lifecycle and retention obligations. | Mandatory |

## 7.2 Definition Principles

Every report definition shall satisfy the following methodological principles before report production may begin.

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose Driven | Every report shall have a clearly defined governance objective. |
| Audience Oriented | Reporting shall be designed according to stakeholder information needs and governance responsibilities. |
| Evidence Aligned | Expected conclusions shall be supported exclusively by validated evidence. |

| Standardized | Report definitions shall follow enterprise reporting standards and approved terminology. |

A formally approved report definition provides the governance baseline against which report quality, completeness, consistency and compliance are evaluated throughout the remaining reporting lifecycle.

# 8. Reporting Inputs and Information Sources

Reporting quality depends on the reliability, completeness and governance of the information used to produce report conclusions.

This framework does not define evidence collection, evidence validation, KPI measurement or decision methodologies. These activities are governed by their respective enterprise frameworks and are incorporated into reporting through controlled information inputs.

## 8.1 Approved Information Sources

Only approved information sources shall be used for enterprise reporting.

| Information Source | Governance Purpose | Primary Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Validated Assessment Results | Provide assessment findings and analytical outputs. | Delivery Activities |
| Validated Evidence | Support reported statements with verified evidence. | DF-008, DF-009 |
| Approved KPI Measurements | Provide measurable performance indicators. | DF-010 |
| Approved Governance Decisions | Support decision reporting and recommendations. | DF-011 |
| Customer-Approved Information | Incorporate validated customer-provided information. | Engagement Records |
| Governance Documentation | Provide policies, standards, procedures and approved methodologies. | Enterprise Governance Documentation |
| Regulatory References | Support regulatory interpretation where applicable. | Applicable Regulations |

## 8.2 Information Source Requirements

Every information source incorporated into a report shall satisfy defined governance requirements before being used within reporting activities.

| Requirement | Description |
|---|---|
| Authority | Information shall originate from an authorized source. |
| Validity | Information shall be current and approved for reporting purposes. |
| Integrity | Information shall remain complete and unaltered throughout the reporting lifecycle. |
| Traceability | Information shall be traceable to its documented origin. |
| Consistency | Information shall be consistent with other approved governance artefacts. |
| Verifiability | Information shall be independently verifiable when required. |

Information that has not been validated, approved or appropriately governed shall not be used as the basis for reporting conclusions.

The Governance Reporting Framework assumes that evidence validation, KPI measurement and governance decision processes have already been performed according to their respective enterprise frameworks before information enters the reporting lifecycle.

# 10. Reporting Rules

Reporting activities shall follow a common set of enterprise rules to ensure consistency, reliability and governance compliance throughout the reporting lifecycle.

These rules apply to every report regardless of service type, organizational context or delivery methodology.

| Reporting Rule | Governance Requirement | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-Based Reporting | Significant statements shall be supported by validated evidence. | Reliable conclusions |
| Objective Reporting | Reports shall distinguish factual findings from interpretations and recommendations. | Decision transparency |
| Consistent Terminology | Approved enterprise terminology shall be used consistently throughout the report. | Information consistency |
| Controlled Assumptions | Assumptions shall be explicitly identified and justified. | Explainable reasoning |
| Documented Limitations | Constraints affecting findings or conclusions shall be clearly documented. | Transparent reporting |
| Authorized Contributions | Only authorized contributors may modify report content. | Content integrity |
| Formal Reviews | Reports shall undergo the required governance reviews before approval. | Quality assurance |
| Version Control | Every revision shall be uniquely identified and traceable. | Lifecycle traceability |
| Controlled Distribution | Reports shall be distributed only to authorized recipients. | Information confidentiality |
| Record Preservation | Reporting records shall be retained according to enterprise retention policies. | Audit readiness |

## 10.1 Mandatory Reporting Practices

The following practices shall be applied throughout all reporting activities:

- report conclusions shall remain consistent with validated evidence;
- governance decisions shall be reported without alteration;
- significant changes shall be documented through controlled version management;
- report ownership shall remain identifiable throughout the reporting lifecycle;
- reporting activities shall preserve explainability, traceability and auditability;
- reporting methodology shall remain independent from implementation technologies and AI providers.

Compliance with these reporting rules is mandatory for every report governed by the Enterprise Delivery Framework and constitutes a prerequisite for formal validation, approval and controlled distribution.

| Version Preservation | Approved report versions shall remain identifiable and retrievable. |
| Record Integrity | Retained reports shall remain complete and unaltered. |
| Controlled Disposal | Report disposal shall follow approved governance procedures after the retention period expires. |
| Audit Availability | Reports required for governance reviews or audits shall remain accessible throughout the applicable retention period. |
| Traceable Archival | Archival activities shall preserve report identity, metadata and lifecycle history. |

## 19.3 Classification and Retention Governance

Report classification and retention decisions shall be documented and governed as part of the official reporting lifecycle.

Changes affecting classification, retention or archival status shall be authorized, documented and fully traceable.

The Governance Reporting Framework defines the governance methodology for classification and retention without prescribing implementation technologies, storage platforms or archival solutions, thereby preserving full technology and provider independence.

# 20. Continuous Improvement

Continuous improvement ensures that reporting governance evolves in response to organizational learning, quality assessments, governance reviews and audit findings while preserving methodological consistency.

Improvement activities shall be evidence-based, measurable and managed through controlled governance processes.

## 20.1 Improvement Drivers

| Improvement Driver | Governance Purpose |
|---|---|
| Quality Reviews | Identify opportunities to enhance reporting quality and consistency. |
| Governance Reviews | Improve reporting governance practices and organizational alignment. |
| Audit Findings | Address identified weaknesses and strengthen reporting controls. |
| Lessons Learned | Capture delivery experience to refine reporting methodologies. |
| KPI Monitoring | Measure reporting performance and identify improvement priorities. |
| Regulatory Evolution | Maintain alignment with applicable governance and regulatory requirements. |

## 20.2 Improvement Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Evidence-Based Improvement | Improvements shall be supported by documented evidence and measurable observations. |
| Controlled Change | Reporting methodology shall evolve through approved governance processes. |
| Measurable Benefits | Improvement initiatives shall define expected governance outcomes and measurable success criteria. |
| Knowledge Preservation | Improvements shall contribute to the continuous evolution of enterprise reporting knowledge. |
| Enterprise Consistency | Improvements shall maintain consistency across all reporting activities governed by the Enterprise Delivery Framework. |

Continuous improvement shall preserve alignment with the Governance First, Evidence First and Measurement First principles while ensuring that reporting governance remains sustainable, repeatable and independent from implementation technologies or AI providers.

# 21. Framework Summary

The Governance Reporting Framework establishes the enterprise methodology governing reporting activities across the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.

The framework defines standardized governance principles, reporting lifecycle activities, quality requirements, responsibilities, validation mechanisms, traceability, explainability, auditability and continuous improvement practices applicable to every enterprise reporting activity.

Together with the Evidence Collection Framework (DF-008), Evidence Validation Process (DF-009), KPI Measurement Framework (DF-010) and Decision Framework (DF-011), this document completes the governance architecture supporting reliable, explainable and auditable reporting without duplicating responsibilities already assigned to those frameworks.

By remaining fully aligned with ADR-CLE-004 — QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle, the framework ensures that enterprise reporting is:

- governance-driven;
- evidence-based;
- measurable;
- explainable;
- traceable;
- auditable;
- standardized;
- technology-independent;
- provider-independent.

This framework provides the methodological foundation upon which enterprise reporting standards, report templates and executive reporting artefacts may be developed while preserving consistency across all Cognitive Logic delivery services.
