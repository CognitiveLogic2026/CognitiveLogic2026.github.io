# 1. Purpose
## 1.1 Purpose Statement

The purpose of this standard is to establish a consistent enterprise methodology for the design, organization, structure, editorial quality, maintenance, identification, and publication readiness of every executive report produced within the Cognitive Logic Delivery Framework.

This document defines the structural and editorial standards that ensure executive reports are understandable, consistent, explainable, traceable, auditable, measurable, and suitable for governance-driven decision-making.

The standard provides a technology-independent framework applicable to all delivery activities regardless of authoring tools, document formats, publishing platforms, or AI providers.

## 1.2 Expected Outcomes

The application of this standard shall ensure:

- consistent report architecture;
- uniform editorial quality;
- standardized document organization;
- clear executive communication;
- reproducible document structure;
- evidence-oriented reporting;
- complete traceability of findings;
- consistent terminology across engagements;
- publication readiness;
- long-term maintainability.

## 1.3 Relationship with Other Framework Documents

This document defines only the editorial and structural standards of executive reports.

It does not define:

- governance decision processes;
- evidence collection methodologies;
- evidence validation;
- KPI measurement;
- governance reporting processes.

Those topics are defined by their respective approved framework documents.
# 2. Scope

## 2.1 Standard Scope

This standard applies to every executive report produced as part of the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.

It establishes the mandatory editorial, structural, documentation, and organizational requirements that shall govern report production throughout the entire delivery lifecycle.

## 2.2 Applicable Deliverables

This standard applies to reports generated for, but not limited to:

- AI Governance Assessments;
- Knowledge Governance Assessments;
- AI Act Readiness Assessments;
- Explainability Assessments;
- Organizational Assessments;
- Enterprise Assessments;
- Risk Assessments;
- Executive Recommendations;
- Governance Reviews;
- Maturity Assessments.

## 2.3 Standard Coverage

This document governs:

- report architecture;
- document organization;
- section hierarchy;
- metadata;
- document identification;
- editorial consistency;
- writing quality;
- terminology consistency;
- evidence referencing;
- cross-references;
- document versioning;
- publication readiness;
- accessibility and readability requirements.

## 2.4 Out of Scope

The following topics are intentionally excluded from this standard:

- governance reporting processes;
- evidence collection methodologies;
- evidence validation procedures;
- KPI measurement methodologies;
- governance decision-making processes;
- software-specific formatting;
- editor-specific templates;
- rendering technologies;
- document management platforms.

## 2.5 Applicability Principles

The requirements defined in this standard shall be applied independently of:

- authoring software;
- publishing platform;
- document format;
- AI provider;
- rendering technology;
- storage repository.

The standard defines enterprise documentation principles rather than implementation-specific rules.

# 4. Executive Reporting Principles

## 4.1 Overview

Executive reports shall communicate governance information in a manner that supports informed, transparent, and evidence-based decision-making.

Every report shall prioritize clarity, consistency, traceability, and executive usability over document volume or visual complexity.

## 4.2 Reporting Principles Matrix

| Principle | Description | Practical Application |
|---|---|---|
| Governance First | Reports support governance decisions. | Present information relevant to decision-makers. |
| Evidence First | Every significant statement is supported by evidence. | Reference validated evidence where applicable. |
| Explainability | Findings shall be understandable by their intended audience. | Avoid unnecessary ambiguity and unexplained assumptions. |
| Traceability | Conclusions shall be traceable to their supporting analysis. | Maintain explicit links between findings and evidence. |
| Consistency | Reports shall follow a standardized structure. | Apply common sections and editorial rules. |
| Accuracy | Information shall be factually correct and verified. | Validate content before publication. |
| Objectivity | Reports shall distinguish facts, observations, interpretations, and recommendations. | Avoid unsupported opinions. |
| Proportionality | The level of detail shall reflect the assessment scope and audience. | Balance completeness with executive readability. |
| Technology Independence | Standards shall not depend on specific software or formats. | Maintain vendor-neutral documentation. |
| Provider Independence | Report quality shall not rely on AI providers or external platforms. | Ensure methodological consistency regardless of implementation. |

## 4.3 Executive Communication Principles

Executive reports shall:

- communicate conclusions before supporting detail;
- distinguish observations from recommendations;
- present risks proportionally;
- avoid unnecessary technical language;
- use consistent terminology throughout the document;
- maintain a logical progression from context to conclusions.

## 4.4 Editorial Integrity

Editorial decisions shall preserve the integrity of the documented evidence.

The presentation of findings shall never alter, exaggerate, omit, or reinterpret validated evidence in a manner that could mislead governance decisions.

## 4.5 Alignment with the Enterprise Delivery Framework

These reporting principles complement the approved Enterprise Delivery Framework by defining how governance outcomes are communicated, without modifying assessment methodologies, evidence management processes, decision frameworks, KPI measurement practices, or governance reporting processes defined in other approved documents.
# 5. Report Architecture

## 5.1 Architecture Overview

Executive reports shall be organized according to a standardized information architecture that promotes consistency, navigation, traceability, and executive readability.

The architecture defines the logical organization of information independently of document formats, publishing technologies, or authoring tools.

## 5.2 Architectural Layers

| Layer | Purpose | Typical Content |
|---|---|---|
| Identification | Identify the report. | Metadata, document identification, version information. |
| Executive Overview | Provide immediate decision support. | Executive Summary, objectives, scope. |
| Assessment Content | Present the analytical results. | Findings, observations, analyses. |
| Decision Support | Support governance decisions. | Recommendations, priorities, proposed actions. |
| Supporting Information | Provide traceability and reference material. | Evidence references, annexes, appendices, glossary. |

## 5.3 Information Flow

Executive reports shall present information using a progressive information flow that moves from high-level conclusions to supporting detail.

The recommended logical sequence is:

1. Identification
2. Executive Context
3. Scope
4. Findings
5. Analysis
6. Recommendations
7. Evidence References
8. Supporting Information

## 5.4 Structural Principles

The report architecture shall ensure:

- logical progression of information;
- clear separation between major sections;
- consistent section hierarchy;
- reusable document organization;
- minimal duplication of information;
- explicit traceability between related sections.

## 5.5 Architectural Integrity

The architectural organization of a report shall remain stable throughout its lifecycle.

Document revisions may modify content but shall preserve the standardized structural organization defined by this standard unless a formally approved revision of the framework establishes otherwise.
# 6. Standard Report Structure

## 6.1 Overview

Every executive report shall follow a standardized structure that ensures consistency, comparability, readability, and governance-oriented communication.

The structure defined in this section represents the enterprise baseline for all executive reports produced within the Cognitive Logic Delivery Framework.

## 6.2 Mandatory Report Sections

| Section | Mandatory | Purpose |
|---|:---:|---|
| Report Identification | Yes | Uniquely identify the document. |
| Executive Summary | Yes | Present the key conclusions for executive readers. |
| Purpose | Yes | State the report objectives. |
| Scope | Yes | Define assessment boundaries. |
| Methodology Overview | Yes | Describe the applied methodology at a high level. |
| Findings | Yes | Present validated observations. |
| Analysis | Yes | Explain the significance of the findings. |
| Recommendations | Yes | Support governance decisions. |
| Evidence References | Yes | Ensure traceability. |
| Conclusion | Yes | Summarize the overall assessment. |
| Revision Information | Yes | Document version history and control. |

## 6.3 Optional Report Sections

| Section | Typical Use |
|---|---|
| Glossary | Domain-specific terminology. |
| Acronyms | Abbreviations used throughout the report. |
| Risk Summary | Executive overview of identified risks. |
| Maturity Overview | High-level maturity assessment. |
| Limitations | Assumptions and assessment constraints. |
| Appendices | Supplementary supporting information. |
| Annexes | Detailed reference material. |

## 6.4 Structural Ordering Rules

Mandatory sections shall appear in a logical sequence that supports executive understanding.

Optional sections may be included when they provide additional value without disrupting the standardized information flow.

## 6.5 Structural Consistency Requirements

Every report shall:

- include all mandatory sections;
- maintain consistent section ordering;
- distinguish mandatory and supporting content;
- preserve logical progression between sections;
- avoid redundant information across multiple sections.

Any justified deviation from the standard structure shall be explicitly documented and approved according to the applicable governance process.
# 7. Section Hierarchy

## 7.1 Hierarchical Principles

Executive reports shall use a consistent hierarchical organization that reflects the logical decomposition of information.

The hierarchy shall improve navigation, readability, traceability, and maintenance throughout the document lifecycle.

## 7.2 Hierarchy Levels

| Level | Purpose | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Report | Entire executive document. | Single controlled document. |
| Chapter | Major logical topic. | Sequentially numbered. |
| Section | Primary subdivision. | Logically grouped content. |
| Subsection | Detailed discussion of a section. | Limited to relevant decomposition. |
| Item | Individual requirement, rule, or statement. | Used only where necessary. |

## 7.3 Hierarchical Consistency Rules

The document hierarchy shall:

- progress from general concepts to detailed information;
- avoid unnecessary nesting;
- maintain consistent numbering throughout the report;
- preserve logical relationships between parent and child sections;
- ensure that every subsection clearly supports its parent section.

## 7.4 Numbering Principles

Section numbering shall be:

- sequential;
- unique within the document;
- stable across revisions whenever possible;
- independent of authoring software;
- applied consistently to equivalent hierarchy levels.

## 7.5 Structural Integrity

Changes to the document shall preserve the established hierarchy.

Structural modifications shall avoid introducing inconsistencies, duplicated numbering, orphan sections, or fragmented information architecture that could reduce executive readability or traceability.

# 9. Naming and Identification Standards

## 9.1 Purpose

Executive reports shall adopt standardized naming and identification rules to ensure uniqueness, consistency, discoverability, and long-term maintainability across the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

Naming conventions shall identify the document without relying on specific technologies, repositories, file systems, or publishing platforms.

## 9.2 Identification Principles

Document identification shall be:

- unique;
- stable throughout the document lifecycle;
- human-readable;
- governance-oriented;
- technology-independent;
- consistent across all report categories.

## 9.3 Naming Convention Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|---|---|
| Clarity | The document name shall clearly describe its purpose. |
| Consistency | Equivalent reports shall follow equivalent naming patterns. |
| Uniqueness | Each controlled report shall have a unique identifier. |
| Stability | Approved document names shall remain stable whenever possible. |
| Neutrality | Names shall avoid references to software, vendors, or implementation technologies. |

## 9.4 Identification Components

A report identification model should include the following logical components where applicable:

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Document Identifier | Unique controlled identifier. |
| Report Title | Official report name. |
| Version | Controlled document version. |
| Status | Lifecycle status. |
| Classification | Information classification level. |

## 9.5 Consistency Rules

Document names and identifiers shall remain consistent across:

- revisions;
- related reports;
- cross-references;
- evidence references;
- governance documentation;
- publication artifacts.

Any modification affecting the official identity of a report shall be managed through the established document control process to preserve traceability and audit consistency.
# 10. Writing Standards

## 10.1 Purpose

Writing standards establish the minimum editorial quality expected for all executive reports produced within the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

The objective is to ensure that reports communicate information accurately, consistently, objectively, and in a manner appropriate for executive decision-making.

## 10.2 Writing Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Information shall be expressed using precise and unambiguous language. | Improved understanding. |
| Accuracy | Statements shall reflect validated information. | Reliable reporting. |
| Objectivity | Personal opinions shall be avoided unless explicitly identified as recommendations. | Neutral communication. |
| Conciseness | Information shall be presented without unnecessary verbosity. | Executive readability. |
| Consistency | Equivalent concepts shall be described using consistent terminology. | Uniform interpretation. |
| Explainability | Findings and conclusions shall be understandable by the intended audience. | Transparent communication. |
| Traceability | Significant statements shall be traceable to supporting evidence where applicable. | Verifiable reporting. |

## 10.3 Writing Requirements

Executive reports shall:

- distinguish facts from interpretations;
- distinguish observations from recommendations;
- use precise and measurable language whenever possible;
- avoid unsupported assumptions;
- avoid speculative statements presented as facts;
- maintain a professional and impartial tone;
- preserve logical continuity between sections.

## 10.4 Statement Classification

| Statement Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Fact | Verifiable information supported by validated sources. |
| Observation | Objective description identified during the assessment. |
| Analysis | Interpretation of one or more observations. |
| Finding | Consolidated assessment result supported by analysis. |
| Recommendation | Proposed action intended to improve governance outcomes. |

Each statement type shall remain clearly distinguishable throughout the report.

## 10.5 Language Consistency

Terminology, abbreviations, defined concepts, and governance vocabulary shall be used consistently throughout the document.

Equivalent concepts shall not be described using multiple alternative expressions unless explicitly defined.

## 10.6 Executive Readability

Reports shall prioritize executive comprehension by:

- presenting conclusions before detailed explanations where appropriate;
- using short and coherent paragraphs;
- avoiding unnecessary repetition;
- maintaining consistent terminology;
- organizing information in a logical sequence that supports governance decision-making.
# 11. Editorial Standards

## 11.1 Purpose

Editorial standards define the rules that ensure consistency, uniformity, and professional quality across all executive reports produced within the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

These standards govern the presentation of information independently of document formats, authoring tools, or publishing technologies.

## 11.2 Editorial Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Uniformity | Reports shall present information consistently across all engagements. | Predictable document organization. |
| Coherence | Related information shall be organized logically. | Improved comprehension. |
| Consistency | Editorial conventions shall be applied uniformly throughout the document. | Professional presentation. |
| Precision | Terminology shall be used consistently and accurately. | Reduced ambiguity. |
| Maintainability | Editorial organization shall facilitate controlled revisions. | Long-term document quality. |

## 11.3 Editorial Rules

Executive reports shall:

- maintain consistent chapter organization;
- apply consistent heading hierarchy;
- use uniform terminology throughout the document;
- avoid duplicated content where possible;
- separate normative statements from explanatory text;
- distinguish informative notes from mandatory requirements.

## 11.4 Terminology Management

Terms having a specific governance meaning shall be defined once and used consistently throughout the report.

Where a glossary is provided, terminology shall remain aligned with the approved definitions contained therein.

Undefined synonyms that may introduce ambiguity should be avoided.

## 11.5 Editorial Quality Review

Before publication, every report should be reviewed to verify:

| Review Area | Verification Objective |
|---|---|
| Terminology | Consistent use of defined terms. |
| Structure | Compliance with the standard report organization. |
| Cross-References | Accuracy of internal references. |
| Consistency | Uniform application of editorial conventions. |
| Completeness | Presence of all mandatory sections. |
| Readability | Suitability for executive audiences. |

## 11.6 Editorial Integrity

Editorial improvements may enhance clarity and readability but shall never modify the meaning, validity, intent, or evidential integrity of the documented assessment results.

Editorial revisions shall preserve the original governance conclusions and maintain full traceability with the supporting evidence.
# 12. Tables and Figures Standards

## 12.1 Purpose

Tables and figures shall improve executive understanding by organizing information in a structured, concise, and easily interpretable manner.

Their use shall enhance communication without replacing the explanatory narrative or supporting evidence.

## 12.2 General Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Every table or figure shall support the report objectives. | Meaningful visual information. |
| Clarity | Information shall be immediately understandable. | Improved executive comprehension. |
| Consistency | Similar information shall be presented using consistent structures. | Uniform interpretation. |
| Accuracy | Values and descriptions shall accurately reflect validated information. | Reliable communication. |
| Traceability | Information shall be traceable to the corresponding report content or evidence where applicable. | Verifiable presentation. |

## 12.3 Tables Standards

Tables shall:

- have a clearly identifiable title;
- present logically organized information;
- use consistent column and row semantics;
- avoid redundant information already explained in the surrounding text;
- remain understandable when viewed independently from the report body.

## 12.4 Figures Standards

Figures, diagrams, charts, and conceptual illustrations shall:

- support executive understanding;
- present information objectively;
- include an informative title;
- be referenced within the report narrative;
- avoid decorative or non-informative content.

## 12.5 Relationship Between Narrative and Visual Elements

Narrative text shall explain the significance of every table or figure.

Tables and figures shall complement the written analysis rather than replace it.

Executive conclusions shall never rely solely on visual representations.

## 12.6 Identification and Referencing

Every table and figure shall be uniquely identifiable within the report.

References to tables and figures shall remain consistent throughout the document and shall preserve traceability during controlled revisions.

## 12.7 Editorial Consistency

Equivalent information shall be presented using equivalent table structures, terminology, and identification rules across all executive reports governed by this standard.
# 13. Evidence Referencing Standards

## 13.1 Purpose

Evidence references establish the connection between executive reporting and the validated information supporting the documented findings.

This standard defines how evidence shall be referenced within executive reports without defining evidence collection, validation, governance, or assessment methodologies.

## 13.2 Referencing Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Traceability | Every significant finding shall be traceable to supporting evidence where applicable. | Verifiable conclusions. |
| Consistency | Evidence references shall follow a uniform approach throughout the report. | Predictable navigation. |
| Precision | References shall uniquely identify the supporting evidence. | Reduced ambiguity. |
| Completeness | Referenced evidence shall be sufficient to support the related statement. | Reliable executive reporting. |
| Independence | References shall remain valid independently of document formats or technologies. | Technology-neutral traceability. |

## 13.3 Evidence Reference Requirements

Evidence references shall:

- clearly identify the supporting evidence;
- maintain a direct relationship with the associated finding;
- avoid ambiguous or generic references;
- remain stable throughout controlled document revisions;
- preserve consistency across the entire report.

## 13.4 Relationship Between Findings and Evidence

Executive findings shall identify the evidence supporting the reported conclusions whenever evidence exists.

Recommendations may reference multiple findings and, indirectly, their supporting evidence without duplicating evidence descriptions.

## 13.5 Evidence Reference Quality

Evidence references should be:

| Quality Attribute | Verification Objective |
|---|---|
| Unique | Clearly distinguish one evidence reference from another. |
| Accurate | Identify the correct supporting evidence. |
| Complete | Provide sufficient information for verification. |
| Consistent | Follow the same referencing approach throughout the report. |
| Maintainable | Remain valid across controlled revisions whenever possible. |

## 13.6 Separation of Responsibilities

This standard governs only the editorial representation of evidence references within executive reports.

Evidence identification, collection, validation, quality assessment, governance, and lifecycle management remain governed by the corresponding approved Enterprise Delivery Framework documents.
# 14. Cross-Reference Standards

## 14.1 Purpose

Cross-references establish explicit relationships between related information contained within an executive report or between controlled documents of the Enterprise Delivery Framework.

Their purpose is to improve navigation, maintain consistency, and strengthen traceability without duplicating information.

## 14.2 Cross-Reference Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | References shall identify the intended target unambiguously. | Reliable navigation. |
| Consistency | Equivalent references shall follow a common approach. | Uniform document structure. |
| Traceability | Related information shall be explicitly connected. | Improved governance transparency. |
| Maintainability | References shall remain valid throughout controlled revisions whenever possible. | Long-term document integrity. |
| Technology Independence | References shall not depend on hyperlinks, software features, or publishing platforms. | Vendor-neutral documentation. |

## 14.3 Types of Cross-References

| Reference Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Internal Section | Refer to another section within the same report. |
| Related Report | Refer to another controlled report or framework document. |
| Evidence Reference | Link findings to supporting evidence references. |
| Annex Reference | Direct readers to supplementary information. |
| Appendix Reference | Reference supporting explanatory material. |

## 14.4 Cross-Reference Requirements

Cross-references shall:

- identify the referenced element clearly;
- avoid ambiguous wording;
- remain consistent throughout the document;
- support executive navigation;
- avoid unnecessary duplication of content.

## 14.5 Cross-Reference Quality Review

During editorial review, cross-references should be verified to ensure:

| Verification Area | Objective |
|---|---|
| Correct Target | Every reference identifies the intended element. |
| Consistency | Equivalent references use equivalent conventions. |
| Completeness | Required references are present where appropriate. |
| Validity | References remain applicable after document revisions. |
| Readability | References improve comprehension without interrupting the narrative flow. |

## 14.6 Editorial Responsibility

Cross-references are editorial navigation mechanisms.

They shall facilitate understanding and traceability without modifying the meaning, authority, or governance responsibilities established by the referenced documents.
# 15. Revision and Version Standards

## 15.1 Purpose

Revision and version standards establish a consistent methodology for identifying, documenting, and communicating controlled changes made to executive reports throughout their lifecycle.

These standards ensure that document evolution remains understandable, traceable, and auditable.

## 15.2 Versioning Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Uniqueness | Every controlled version shall be uniquely identified. | Clear document identification. |
| Traceability | Revisions shall preserve the history of document evolution. | Verifiable change history. |
| Consistency | Version information shall follow a common enterprise approach. | Uniform document control. |
| Transparency | Significant revisions shall be documented. | Improved governance visibility. |
| Stability | Published versions shall remain identifiable over time. | Reliable document referencing. |

## 15.3 Revision History

Executive reports should maintain a controlled revision history containing, where applicable:

| Revision Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Version Identifier | Identify the released revision. |
| Revision Date | Record when the revision was issued. |
| Revision Summary | Describe the principal changes introduced. |
| Author | Identify the document author or responsible contributor. |
| Approval Status | Record the applicable document status. |

## 15.4 Version Management Requirements

Version information shall:

- remain consistent throughout the document;
- accurately represent the current controlled revision;
- distinguish draft and approved versions;
- support historical traceability;
- avoid ambiguous or conflicting identifiers.

## 15.5 Controlled Changes

Editorial revisions shall preserve document integrity.

Changes affecting findings, recommendations, evidence references, or governance conclusions shall be reflected through the appropriate controlled revision process while maintaining complete traceability between document versions.

## 15.6 Relationship with Document Control

This standard defines the documentary representation of revisions and versions.

Approval workflows, governance responsibilities, change authorization, and lifecycle management remain governed by the applicable Enterprise Delivery Framework documentation.
# 16. Readability and Accessibility

## 16.1 Purpose

Executive reports shall be designed to maximize readability, comprehension, and accessibility for their intended audience.

These standards promote effective communication independently of document formats, presentation technologies, or publication platforms.

## 16.2 Readability Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Information shall be communicated using clear and precise language. | Rapid executive understanding. |
| Logical Flow | Information shall follow a coherent progression. | Improved comprehension. |
| Consistency | Similar concepts shall be presented consistently throughout the report. | Predictable reading experience. |
| Conciseness | Content shall avoid unnecessary complexity and repetition. | Efficient executive review. |
| Completeness | Essential information shall be presented without omitting relevant context. | Reliable decision support. |

## 16.3 Accessibility Principles

Executive reports shall:

- use meaningful and descriptive section titles;
- organize information using a consistent hierarchy;
- distinguish clearly between mandatory and supporting information;
- avoid unnecessary jargon whenever equivalent business terminology is available;
- support readers with different levels of governance expertise.

## 16.4 Information Organization

Information shall be organized to facilitate:

- rapid executive review;
- progressive understanding;
- efficient navigation;
- identification of key findings;
- interpretation of recommendations;
- verification of supporting references.

## 16.5 Readability Quality Criteria

| Quality Attribute | Verification Objective |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Statements are immediately understandable. |
| Consistency | Terminology and structure remain uniform. |
| Logical Sequence | Topics progress naturally from one section to the next. |
| Executive Orientation | Information supports governance decision-making. |
| Accessibility | Information can be understood without unnecessary technical barriers. |

## 16.6 Continuous Readability

Editorial revisions shall preserve or improve readability.

Changes that increase ambiguity, reduce consistency, fragment the information flow, or impair executive understanding should be avoided throughout the document lifecycle.
# 17. Publication Readiness

## 17.1 Purpose

Publication readiness defines the minimum editorial, structural, and documentation quality required before an executive report is considered suitable for controlled distribution.

Publication readiness does not constitute governance approval and shall not replace the applicable review or authorization processes.

## 17.2 Readiness Principles

| Principle | Description | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Completeness | All mandatory sections are present. | Complete documentation. |
| Consistency | Editorial and structural standards have been applied uniformly. | Professional presentation. |
| Accuracy | Content has been verified for correctness. | Reliable executive communication. |
| Traceability | Findings, recommendations, and references are properly connected. | Verifiable reporting. |
| Readiness | The report satisfies the applicable documentation standards. | Controlled publication. |

## 17.3 Publication Checklist

Before publication, the report should be verified for:

| Verification Area | Objective |
|---|---|
| Metadata | Mandatory metadata are complete and consistent. |
| Structure | Mandatory sections are present and correctly organized. |
| Editorial Quality | Writing and editorial standards have been applied. |
| Cross-References | Internal and external references are correct. |
| Evidence References | Supporting evidence references are complete and consistent. |
| Revision Information | Version and revision history accurately represent the current document state. |

## 17.4 Publication Integrity

A report shall be considered publication-ready only when it satisfies the structural, editorial, and documentation requirements established by this standard.

Governance approval remains subject to the applicable Enterprise Delivery Framework processes.
