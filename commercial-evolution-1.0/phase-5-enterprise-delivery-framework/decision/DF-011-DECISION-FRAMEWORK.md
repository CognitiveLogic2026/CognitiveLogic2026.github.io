# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this framework is to establish the enterprise methodology governing the complete lifecycle of decisions produced during Cognitive Logic delivery activities.

The framework defines how decisions are initiated, evaluated, validated, approved, documented, reviewed and continuously improved while ensuring consistency, explainability, traceability, auditability and governance across all enterprise services.

This framework is technology-independent, provider-independent and fully aligned with the QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle (ADR-CLE-004).
# 2. Scope

This framework applies to every decision generated, supported, reviewed or approved during the delivery of Cognitive Logic enterprise services.

The methodology is applicable to:

- AI Governance
- Knowledge Governance
- AI Act Readiness Assessments
- Explainability Assessments
- Risk Assessments
- Enterprise Assessments
- Organizational Assessments

The framework governs methodological decision processes only and does not prescribe specific technologies, software platforms, implementation architectures or AI providers.

Its scope includes:

- Decision governance
- Decision ownership
- Decision authorities
- Decision responsibilities
- Decision lifecycle
- Decision quality
- Decision evidence
- Decision traceability
- Decision explainability
- Decision auditability
- Decision reporting
- Continuous decision improvement

The framework complements, without replacing or duplicating:

- DF-008 — Evidence Collection Framework
- DF-009 — Evidence Validation Process
- DF-010 — KPI Measurement Framework

Evidence collection, evidence validation and KPI measurement remain governed by their respective frameworks, while this document defines how validated information is governed throughout the enterprise decision process.

# 4. Decision Governance Principles

Decision governance shall ensure that every enterprise decision is:

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Governance First | Decisions shall follow approved governance processes before execution. |
| Evidence First | Decisions shall rely exclusively on validated and documented evidence. |
| Explainability | Every decision shall be understandable and justifiable. |
| Traceability | Every decision shall be traceable from initiation to closure. |
| Accountability | Decision ownership shall always be explicitly assigned. |
| Consistency | Similar situations shall follow consistent decision methodologies. |
| Transparency | Decision rationale shall be documented and reviewable. |
| Risk Awareness | Risks shall be evaluated before decision approval. |
| Proportionality | Decision rigor shall be proportional to business impact and risk. |
| Continuous Improvement | Decision quality shall improve through systematic review and lessons learned. |
| Technology Independence | Decision methodologies shall not depend on specific technologies or software platforms. |
| Provider Independence | Decision methodologies shall remain independent from AI providers, cloud vendors and implementation tools. |
| Sovereign Intelligence | Decisions shall comply with ADR-CLE-004 by relying on intelligible information, validated evidence and sovereign governance principles. |

These principles apply throughout the complete decision lifecycle and constitute mandatory governance requirements for all Cognitive Logic delivery services.
# 5. Decision Lifecycle

The enterprise decision lifecycle defines the standardized stages governing every decision from initiation to continuous improvement.

| Phase | Objective | Primary Output |
|---|---|---|
| Decision Identification | Recognize the need for a decision. | Decision Request |
| Decision Definition | Define scope, objectives, assumptions and constraints. | Decision Definition Record |
| Information Assessment | Review validated inputs and supporting evidence. | Decision Input Set |
| Alternative Evaluation | Identify and compare feasible alternatives. | Alternative Analysis |
| Risk and Impact Assessment | Evaluate business, governance and compliance impacts. | Impact Assessment |
| Decision Formulation | Select the preferred decision based on established criteria. | Proposed Decision |
| Approval | Obtain required governance approvals. | Approved Decision |
| Implementation Authorization | Authorize execution of the approved decision. | Execution Authorization |
| Monitoring | Observe implementation outcomes and expected results. | Decision Monitoring Record |
| Review and Improvement | Evaluate effectiveness and identify improvements. | Lessons Learned |

Each lifecycle phase shall produce documented outputs supporting explainability, traceability, accountability and auditability.

Progression to the next phase shall occur only when the governance requirements of the current phase have been satisfied.
# 6. Decision Taxonomy and Hierarchy

Enterprise decisions shall be classified according to their governance scope, business impact and approval authority.

## 6.1 Decision Taxonomy

| Decision Type | Purpose | Typical Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Decision | Defines long-term governance direction. | Enterprise |
| Tactical Decision | Supports planning and coordination activities. | Cross-Service |
| Operational Decision | Governs execution activities. | Service |
| Compliance Decision | Ensures regulatory and policy conformity. | Governance |
| Risk Decision | Addresses identified risks and mitigation actions. | Risk Management |
| Quality Decision | Supports quality assurance and continuous improvement. | Quality Management |
| Exception Decision | Approves justified deviations from standard processes. | Controlled Exception |

## 6.2 Decision Hierarchy

| Level | Scope | Typical Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | Organization-wide decisions. | Executive Governance |
| Programme | Multi-service governance decisions. | Programme Authority |
| Service | Individual service delivery decisions. | Service Owner |
| Activity | Execution-level decisions. | Delivery Lead |
| Task | Operational task decisions. | Assigned Responsible Role |

Decision authority shall increase proportionally with decision impact, organizational scope, regulatory exposure and associated risk.

Lower-level decisions shall not override decisions approved at higher governance levels unless formally authorized through the established escalation process.
# 7. Decision Definition Methodology

Every enterprise decision shall be formally defined before evaluation and approval.

## 7.1 Mandatory Decision Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Decision Identifier | Unique identifier assigned to the decision. |
| Decision Title | Concise description of the decision. |
| Business Objective | Expected business outcome. |
| Decision Scope | Organizational scope affected by the decision. |
| Decision Owner | Accountable owner of the decision. |
| Decision Type | Classification according to the approved taxonomy. |
| Priority | Relative importance of the decision. |
| Assumptions | Documented assumptions supporting the decision. |
| Constraints | Business, regulatory or operational limitations. |
| Dependencies | Related decisions, activities or governance requirements. |
| Expected Outcomes | Intended measurable results. |

## 7.2 Decision Definition Requirements

Every decision definition shall:

- clearly describe the decision objective;
- identify the business context;
- specify the governance scope;
- identify all relevant stakeholders;
- document assumptions and constraints;
- define measurable expected outcomes;
- identify dependencies affecting the decision;
- support subsequent validation, review and audit activities.

# 8. Decision Inputs and Evidence

Enterprise decisions shall be supported by validated information and documented inputs originating from approved governance activities.

This framework governs the use of decision inputs, while evidence collection and evidence validation remain governed by DF-008 and DF-009.

## 8.1 Decision Inputs

| Input Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Business Requirements | Business objectives and stakeholder expectations. |
| Validated Evidence | Evidence approved according to the Evidence Validation Process. |
| Assessment Results | Outputs generated by approved assessment methodologies. |
| Risk Information | Identified risks and mitigation analyses. |
| Compliance Information | Regulatory, contractual and policy requirements. |
| KPI Information | Performance indicators measured under DF-010. |
| Expert Judgement | Documented specialist evaluations and recommendations. |
| Previous Decisions | Relevant historical decisions and precedents. |

## 8.2 Evidence Utilization Principles

Decision inputs shall satisfy the following principles:

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Inputs shall directly support the decision objective. |
| Validity | Inputs shall originate from validated sources. |
| Completeness | Sufficient information shall be available before approval. |
| Consistency | Inputs shall not contain unresolved contradictions. |
| Traceability | Every input shall be traceable to its documented source. |
| Timeliness | Information shall be appropriate for the decision context. |

Decisions shall not rely on undocumented assumptions, unverifiable information or evidence that has not completed the approved validation process.
# 9. Decision Criteria Framework

Enterprise decisions shall be evaluated using documented, objective and repeatable decision criteria.

Decision criteria shall be established before alternatives are assessed and shall remain consistent throughout the evaluation process.

## 9.1 Decision Criteria Categories

| Criteria Category | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Strategic Alignment | Alignment with organizational objectives and governance strategy. |
| Business Value | Expected contribution to business outcomes. |
| Evidence Strength | Quality and reliability of supporting evidence. |
| Risk Exposure | Potential business, operational and compliance risks. |
| Regulatory Compliance | Conformity with applicable regulations and internal policies. |
| Feasibility | Practical capability to implement the decision. |
| Resource Impact | Expected use of organizational resources. |
| Stakeholder Impact | Effects on internal and external stakeholders. |
| Sustainability | Long-term organizational viability and maintainability. |

## 9.2 Decision Evaluation Principles

Decision criteria shall be:

- objective;
- documented;
- measurable whenever applicable;
- consistently applied;
- proportional to decision impact;
- reviewable during audits;
- explainable to stakeholders.

Any deviation from established decision criteria shall be documented, justified and approved according to the defined governance process.
# 10. Decision Rules

Decision rules define the mandatory governance conditions that regulate how enterprise decisions shall be evaluated, approved and maintained throughout their lifecycle.

## 10.1 Decision Rule Categories

| Rule Category | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Governance Rules | Ensure compliance with approved governance policies. |
| Evidence Rules | Require validated evidence before decision approval. |
| Approval Rules | Define mandatory approval authorities. |
| Risk Rules | Ensure identified risks are evaluated before approval. |
| Compliance Rules | Ensure conformity with regulatory and internal requirements. |
| Quality Rules | Protect decision quality and consistency. |
| Documentation Rules | Ensure complete decision documentation. |
| Review Rules | Define mandatory review conditions and review frequency. |

## 10.2 Mandatory Decision Rules

Every enterprise decision shall:

- have a clearly identified owner;
- reference validated decision inputs;
- follow the approved decision lifecycle;
- apply documented evaluation criteria;
- identify significant risks and expected impacts;
- document considered alternatives when applicable;
- obtain the required approval authority;
- maintain complete traceability of changes;
- support independent review and audit.

## 10.3 Decision Constraints

Decision constraints shall be explicitly documented whenever they influence the decision outcome.

Typical constraints include:

| Constraint Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Regulatory | Laws, regulations and contractual obligations. |
| Organizational | Internal policies, governance models and operating procedures. |
| Resource | Budget, personnel, skills and time availability. |
| Technical | Existing enterprise architecture and operational limitations. |
| Operational | Business continuity, service delivery and execution capacity. |

Constraints shall be reviewed during decision validation to ensure they remain applicable and appropriately documented.

# 12. Decision Approval and Escalation

Enterprise decisions shall follow a documented approval process appropriate to their scope, impact and associated risk.

## 12.1 Approval Principles

Every decision approval process shall ensure:

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Appropriate Authority | Approval shall be granted only by the designated authority. |
| Evidence-Based Approval | Approval shall rely on validated evidence and documented evaluation. |
| Independence | Reviews shall remain independent from decision ownership where appropriate. |
| Accountability | Approval responsibilities shall be explicitly assigned. |
| Traceability | Approval actions shall be fully documented. |
| Timeliness | Decisions shall be approved within defined governance expectations. |

## 12.2 Approval Levels

| Decision Impact | Typical Approval Level |
|---|---|
| Low | Delivery Authority |
| Moderate | Service Governance |
| High | Programme Governance |
| Critical | Executive Governance |

Approval levels may be adjusted according to regulatory obligations, organizational policies and delegated governance authority.

## 12.3 Escalation Conditions

Escalation shall occur when one or more of the following conditions exist:

- unresolved governance conflicts;
- insufficient or contradictory validated evidence;
- unacceptable risk exposure;
- significant regulatory or compliance implications;
- enterprise-wide business impact;
- unresolved stakeholder disagreement;
- approval authority exceeds delegated responsibilities.

## 12.4 Escalation Principles

Escalated decisions shall:

- preserve complete decision history;
- retain documented rationale for escalation;
- identify the receiving approval authority;
- record all additional reviews performed;
- maintain full traceability from the original decision request through final approval.

Escalation shall strengthen governance oversight without compromising decision transparency, explainability or accountability.
# 13. Decision Validation

Decision validation confirms that an enterprise decision satisfies governance requirements before implementation authorization.

Decision validation does not replace evidence validation. Instead, it verifies that validated evidence has been correctly interpreted and applied throughout the decision process.

## 13.1 Validation Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| Completeness | Verify that all mandatory decision components are documented. |
| Consistency | Ensure alignment between objectives, criteria, evidence and outcomes. |
| Governance Compliance | Confirm adherence to approved governance principles. |
| Evidence Utilization | Verify that only validated evidence supports the decision. |
| Risk Adequacy | Confirm that identified risks have been appropriately evaluated. |
| Explainability | Ensure the decision rationale can be clearly communicated. |
| Traceability | Verify complete linkage between inputs, evaluation and decision outcome. |

## 13.2 Validation Checklist

Every decision validation should confirm that:

- the decision objective is clearly defined;
- decision ownership is assigned;
- the decision taxonomy is correctly applied;
- required inputs have been documented;
- validated evidence supports the decision;
- evaluation criteria have been consistently applied;
- identified risks have been assessed;
- approval requirements have been satisfied;
- expected outcomes have been documented;
- traceability information is complete.

## 13.3 Validation Outcomes

| Outcome | Description |
|---|---|
| Validated | Decision satisfies all governance requirements. |
| Validated with Recommendations | Decision is acceptable but includes documented improvement recommendations. |
| Validation Pending | Additional information or review is required before approval. |
| Rejected | Decision does not satisfy mandatory governance requirements. |

Only validated decisions shall proceed to implementation authorization unless a formally approved exception process applies.

# 15. Decision Traceability

Decision traceability ensures that every enterprise decision can be reconstructed, reviewed and justified throughout its entire lifecycle.

Traceability establishes verifiable relationships between decision objectives, supporting inputs, validated evidence, evaluation activities, approvals, implementation outcomes and subsequent reviews.

## 15.1 Traceability Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| End-to-End Visibility | Enable complete visibility across the decision lifecycle. |
| Decision Reconstruction | Allow independent reconstruction of the decision process. |
| Governance Assurance | Demonstrate compliance with approved governance principles. |
| Accountability | Identify responsibilities for every decision activity. |
| Audit Support | Facilitate internal and external audit activities. |
| Continuous Learning | Preserve decision knowledge for future reference and improvement. |

## 15.2 Traceability Relationships

Every decision shall maintain documented relationships with:

| Traceability Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Business Objectives | Identify the organizational purpose of the decision. |
| Decision Inputs | Link all documented information supporting the decision. |
| Validated Evidence | Reference approved evidence used during evaluation. |
| Evaluation Criteria | Identify the criteria applied during assessment. |
| Risk Assessments | Link associated risk analyses and mitigation actions. |
| Approval Records | Preserve approval history and delegated authority. |
| Implementation Activities | Connect approved decisions to execution activities. |
| Review Records | Record subsequent evaluations and improvement actions. |

## 15.3 Traceability Principles

Decision traceability shall ensure that every documented relationship is:

- uniquely identifiable;
- complete;
- verifiable;
- consistently maintained;
- reviewable;
- audit-ready;
- preserved throughout the decision lifecycle.

Loss of traceability shall be treated as a governance issue requiring formal review and corrective action.
# 16. Decision Explainability

Decision explainability ensures that enterprise decisions can be clearly understood, justified and communicated to all relevant stakeholders according to their governance responsibilities.

Explainability enables confidence in decision outcomes by documenting not only what decision was made, but also why it was made and how the conclusion was reached.

## 16.1 Explainability Objectives

|---|---|

| Stakeholder Understanding | Enable appropriate understanding by different stakeholder groups. |
| Governance Confidence | Support confidence in governance processes and decision quality. |
| Audit Readiness | Facilitate independent review of decision reasoning. |
| Knowledge Preservation | Retain organizational knowledge supporting future decisions. |

## 16.2 Explainability Components

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Decision Context | Describe the business situation requiring the decision. |
| Decision Objective | State the intended outcome. |
| Supporting Evidence | Reference the validated evidence considered. |
| Evaluation Criteria | Identify the criteria applied during evaluation. |

| Expected Outcomes | Describe the anticipated organizational benefits. |

## 16.3 Explainability Principles

Enterprise decision explainability shall ensure that every decision is:

- understandable;
- evidence-based;
- logically consistent;
- proportionate to decision complexity;
- supported by documented rationale;
- suitable for independent review;
- aligned with the QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle.

Explainability shall be maintained throughout the complete decision lifecycle, including reviews, revisions and subsequent decision versions.

# 21. Framework Summary

The Decision Framework establishes the enterprise methodology governing decision definition, evaluation, approval, validation, documentation, traceability, explainability, auditability and continuous improvement across all Cognitive Logic delivery services.

The framework ensures that enterprise decisions are:

- governance-driven;
- evidence-based;
- measurable;
- explainable;
- traceable;
- auditable;
- accountable;
- consistently documented;
- technology-independent;
- provider-independent;
- aligned with the QEN Sovereign Intelligence Principle.

Together with DF-008 (Evidence Collection Framework), DF-009 (Evidence Validation Process) and DF-010 (KPI Measurement Framework), this framework completes the methodological governance required for transparent, verifiable and repeatable enterprise decision management throughout the Cognitive Logic Enterprise Delivery Framework.
Incomplete decision definitions shall not proceed to evaluation or approval.
