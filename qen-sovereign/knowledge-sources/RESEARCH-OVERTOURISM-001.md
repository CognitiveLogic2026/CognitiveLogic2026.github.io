## Executive Summary

Digital overtourism describes the systematic concentration of destination visibility inside digital recommendation environments before a physical journey begins.

Generative AI and tourism recommendation systems may repeatedly amplify destinations, attractions and operators that are already strongly represented online.

Current evidence supports the existence of popularity bias, recurrent destination concentration and unequal representation in tourism recommendation environments.

However, a general causal relationship between AI recommendations and increased physical overtourism has not been established.

The evidence chain must therefore remain explicit:

digital representation → algorithmic selection → recommendation → exposure → intention → choice → travel → territorial pressure.

Each stage requires separate measurement.

## The claim

Generative AI and tourism recommendation systems may amplify existing digital visibility by repeatedly recommending destinations, attractions and businesses that are already strongly represented online.

If this occurs systematically, AI may concentrate attention before a physical journey begins.

This does not establish that AI causes physical overtourism.

## Operational definition

Digital overtourism is the systematic concentration of destination visibility inside digital recommendation environments, narrowing the range of places travellers encounter before making a decision.

Traditional overtourism concerns physical pressure on territories.

Digital overtourism concerns the distribution of attention during the pre-travel phase.

## Evidence of concentration

Research in tourism recommendation environments indicates that apparently diverse recommendation outputs may repeatedly converge on a relatively narrow set of destinations.

The number of distinct destinations mentioned at least once is not equivalent to effective diversity of exposure.

System-level distribution across repeated prompts is therefore a more relevant measure than diversity inside a single answer.

## Popularity bias

Popularity bias in tourism recommendation systems predates generative AI.

Destinations and points of interest with stronger visibility signals may have a higher probability of being recommended.

Less represented places may consequently remain underexposed even when relevant to travellers.

## Generative AI and representation

Traditional search engines rank documents.

Generative AI constructs answers and decides which destinations to name, omit or associate with concepts such as:

- authenticity;
- sustainability;
- culture;
- affordability;
- tranquillity.

The distribution of representation therefore becomes a measurable governance object.

## Visibility feedback loop

A possible recursive cycle is:

visibility → recommendation → attention → experience → new content → greater visibility.

Destinations with more reviews, guides, indexed pages, structured information and editorial coverage may become easier for recommendation systems to recognise and reproduce.

This effect does not require intentional bias.

Unequal information environments may be sufficient.

## AI and demand dispersion

AI recommendation systems may also support dispersion.

They can be designed to suggest:

- less congested areas;
- alternative itineraries;
- different time windows;
- underrepresented destinations.

AI may therefore amplify concentration or support redistribution.

The outcome depends on:

- data;
- ranking objectives;
- recommendation design;
- territorial indicators incorporated into the system.

## What destinations should measure

Relevant measurements include:

### Recommendation frequency

How often each destination, municipality, attraction or operator appears.

### Effective diversity

How many places receive a meaningful share of total exposure.

### Geographic concentration

How recommendations are spatially distributed across the territory.

### Prompt sensitivity

Whether destination representation changes across different user intents.

### Evidence representation

Which institutional pages, datasets, structured content and authoritative sources make parts of a territory easier for AI systems to interpret.

## Evidence governance

Digital overtourism can be examined through four governance dimensions:

### Representation

Which places enter the information environment.

### Evidence

Which verifiable sources support their representation.

### Distribution

How attention is distributed among territories and operators.

### Accountability

Whether resulting concentration can be measured and audited.

## Claims boundary

The central claim is classified as partially verified.

Current evidence supports:

- popularity bias;
- recurrent destination concentration;
- unequal representation;
- concentrated pre-travel recommendation patterns.

Current evidence does not generally establish:

AI recommendation → increase in physical overtourism.

Demonstrating that relationship would require longitudinal evidence connecting:

- algorithmic exposure;
- travel intentions;
- bookings;
- visitor movements;
- measurable territorial pressure.

## Human and institutional interpretation

Recommendation concentration should not be interpreted as proof of physical tourism pressure without additional evidence.

Destination managers, public authorities and tourism organizations retain responsibility for interpreting digital visibility metrics in territorial context.

## Limitations

This source evaluates digital representation and recommendation concentration.

It does not establish a universal causal relationship between generative AI recommendations and physical overtourism.

It does not constitute:

- destination certification;
- causal impact assessment;
- regulatory determination;
- legal advice;
- proof that algorithmic visibility directly produces visitor pressure.
