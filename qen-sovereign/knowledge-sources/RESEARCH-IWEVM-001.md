## Executive Summary

International Watch Evidence Verification Methodology (IW-EVM) establishes a reproducible and controlled path from a bounded claim to an auditable conclusion.

The methodology keeps claims, evidence, interpretation and operational recommendations distinct so that each passage can be reviewed independently.

IW-EVM supports evidence governance by requiring source provenance, relevance and temporal validity, triangulation, explicit confidence and limitations, reconstructable decision records and preservation of human authority.

Its purpose is not to declare absolute truth, but to make the evidentiary path behind a conclusion inspectable, bounded and auditable.

## Purpose

IW-EVM establishes a controlled path from a bounded claim to an auditable conclusion.

It separates:

- claims;
- evidence;
- interpretation;
- operational recommendations.

This separation allows each component to be examined independently.

## Verification dimensions

The methodology evaluates:

- source provenance and authenticity;
- relevance and temporal validity;
- triangulation and management of conflicting evidence;
- confidence assessment;
- explicit limitations;
- decision records;
- reconstructable audit trails;
- preservation of human authority;
- accountability.

## Claims boundary

A conclusion must not exceed what the available evidence actually establishes.

Authoritative provenance alone does not guarantee that a downstream claim is adequately supported.

Claims must remain bounded by:

- the scope of the underlying source;
- the period covered by the evidence;
- methodological limitations;
- unresolved conflicts;
- the confidence justified by the available evidence.

## Evidence and provenance

Evidence should remain connected to its source, context and verification status.

Relevant properties include:

- origin;
- authenticity;
- publication or observation date;
- methodological scope;
- authority;
- relevance;
- limitations;
- integrity.

## Decision traceability

IW-EVM supports reconstructable decision records and audit trails.

A reviewer should be able to determine:

- which claim was evaluated;
- which evidence was considered;
- how evidence was interpreted;
- what limitations were known;
- how confidence was assessed;
- which conclusion was reached;
- which human authority retained responsibility.

## Human authority

IW-EVM preserves human authority and accountability.

The methodology supports analysis and evidence verification but does not transfer final decision authority to an automated system.

## Limitations

IW-EVM supports evidence governance and accountable decision-making.

It does not constitute:

- legal advice;
- statutory certification;
- accredited audit;
- peer review.

The methodology does not certify the truth of a claim. It structures the process through which evidence, interpretation, uncertainty and decision responsibility can be examined.
