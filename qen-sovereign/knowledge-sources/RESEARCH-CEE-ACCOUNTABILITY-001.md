## Executive Summary

AI capability does not automatically create accountable AI capability.

Investment, compute, skills and regulatory certainty can accelerate AI adoption across Central and Eastern Europe, but consequential AI deployment creates an additional requirement: organisations must be able to reconstruct how an AI-assisted decision was reached.

The operational layer between AI capability and accountable decisions includes:

- governed sources;
- distinction between retrieved information and machine inference;
- visible uncertainty and limitations;
- decision evidence;
- identifiable human authority;
- reconstructable decisions.

The central evidence chain is:

SOURCE → INFERENCE → EVIDENCE → HUMAN AUTHORITY → DECISION.

Evidence governance does not replace investment, compute, skills or regulatory certainty. It makes deployed capability more governable.

## From rules to capability

The CEE AI Challenger 2026 identifies structural requirements for stronger AI development and adoption, including:

- capital;
- computing infrastructure;
- skills;
- faster deployment;
- regulatory coherence.

Regulation establishes conditions.

The next challenge is building organisational and economic capability to deploy AI productively.

## Adoption is not the final measure

AI adoption alone does not establish transformation or accountable use.

When AI enters operational processes, organisations need to understand:

- which sources supported a conclusion;
- which information was excluded;
- what the system inferred rather than retrieved;
- where uncertainty entered the process;
- which model or provider participated;
- whether a human reviewed the conclusion;
- who authorised the resulting action.

These are questions about decision evidence rather than adoption alone.

## Compliance documentation and decision evidence

Compliance documentation and decision evidence solve different problems.

Compliance documentation asks:

Was the system governed according to applicable requirements?

Decision evidence asks:

Can the organisation reconstruct why a particular conclusion entered a particular decision?

A policy may be documented, a model classified and a provider approved while an individual decision remains difficult to reconstruct.

## Evidence chain

A minimal evidence chain is:

SOURCE → INFERENCE → EVIDENCE → HUMAN AUTHORITY → DECISION.

### Source

What information entered the reasoning process.

### Inference

What the AI derived rather than retrieved directly.

### Evidence

Which elements were considered sufficiently reliable and relevant to support action.

### Human authority

Who had authority to accept, reject or modify the AI-assisted conclusion.

### Decision

What action was ultimately taken.

The objective is not to preserve every token generated by every model.

The objective is to preserve what must remain reconstructable when an AI output becomes consequential.

## Accountable AI capability

An organisation may possess AI capability without yet possessing accountable AI capability.

A useful operational minimum is whether the organisation can answer five questions:

1. Can it identify the sources behind a consequential AI conclusion?
2. Can it distinguish retrieved information from machine inference?
3. Can uncertainty, limitations or conflicting evidence remain visible?
4. Is the point of human authority identifiable?
5. Can the decision be reconstructed afterwards without depending exclusively on the AI provider?

## Industrial example

In an AI-supported preventive-maintenance process, the final recommendation alone is an output.

Decision evidence requires preserving the chain connecting:

- sensor data;
- maintenance records;
- inference;
- uncertainty;
- validation;
- human authority;
- resulting action.

## CEE adoption and accountability

Central and Eastern Europe is seeking faster AI adoption across industry, public administration and other sectors.

The research proposition is that evidence governance can be incorporated while adoption is accelerating rather than added only after AI becomes deeply embedded in organisational processes.

The progression is:

RULES → CAPABILITY → DEPLOYMENT → EVIDENCE → ACCOUNTABLE DECISIONS.

Adoption and accountability do not have to compete.

## Claims boundary

This source argues that AI deployment creates governance requirements beyond adoption and capability.

It does not claim that evidence governance alone guarantees safe, lawful or successful AI deployment.

It does not claim that compliance documentation is unnecessary.

It distinguishes compliance documentation from evidence relating to an individual AI-assisted decision.

It does not certify organisations, providers, models or jurisdictions as accountable.

It does not establish that all AI decisions require identical levels of evidence preservation.

## Human authority

Human authority remains part of the decision chain.

For consequential AI-assisted decisions, an identifiable human authority should retain the ability and responsibility to accept, reject, modify or authorise the resulting action.
