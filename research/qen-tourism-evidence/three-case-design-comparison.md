# QEN Tourism Evidence Assessment
## Design Comparison — Rimini, Riccione and Albenga

**Comparison date:** 12 October 2026
**Status:** INTERNAL EVIDENCE COMPARISON
**Ranking status:** NOT SCORED
**QEN weight status:** UNCHANGED
**Publication status:** Internal research; not approved for publication.

## Purpose

This comparison identifies what can currently be verified about the design
and documentary architecture of three municipal beach-concession
procedures.

It does not compare municipalities as better or worse and does not assess
economic operators.

## Evidence profiles

### Rimini

The verified material provides a complete high-level scoring architecture:

- 100 available points;
- 90 technical points;
- 10 economic points;
- 45-point technical threshold;
- 26 discretionary technical points;
- 64 tabular technical points.

Rimini is therefore usable for analysis of the relationship between
technical evaluation, economic evaluation and exclusion threshold.

This statement concerns the type of evidence available in the dossier, not
the quality of the municipality or the final procedure.

### Riccione

The verified material currently concerns:

- a procedure for 14 concessions;
- five official clarifications;
- the economic contribution;
- management experience;
- bidder-specific income;
- documentary attachments;
- exclusion of avvalimento.

Riccione is therefore usable for analysing how official clarifications
change the practical interpretation of participation requirements.

The complete scoring matrix has not been reconstructed in this dossier.

### Albenga

The verified material covers two procedures and eight primary documents.

The reconstruction contains:

- 49 criterion records;
- 100 available points for PUD 19;
- 100 available points for PUD 34;
- commissioner coefficients;
- fixed time thresholds;
- documentary criteria;
- an explicit relationship between investment, PEF, requested duration and
  municipal congruity review.

The interpretive QEN mapping records:

- 21 Territorial-primary criteria;
- 17 Social-primary criteria;
- 11 Environmental-primary criteria.

These counts measure classification coverage only. They are not QEN
weights, scores or evidence of implementation.

## Material findings

### 1. The cases are complementary

The three records expose different parts of procedural governance:

- Rimini: scoring structure;
- Riccione: interpretive effect of official clarifications;
- Albenga: criterion-level design and investment-duration congruity.

No single record currently contains the complete sequence from tender
design through submitted evidence, award and implementation.

### 2. Point totals are not comparable performance scores

Rimini and the two Albenga procedures use 100-point architectures, but the
distribution, objects and evaluation methods differ.

The common total does not create a valid municipal ranking.

### 3. Evidence maturity must remain dimension-specific

A procedure may be well documented in one area and incomplete in another.

For that reason, evidence states must be attached to individual dimensions
rather than collapsed into one overall score.

### 4. Design does not prove implementation

Published criteria and commitments do not establish:

- authenticity of bidder evidence;
- quality of commission reasoning;
- implementation of awarded commitments;
- environmental or social outcomes;
- value generated for the territory.

Those stages require later evidence.

## Methodological limits

- The concession objects are not identical.
- The procedures are at different documentary and temporal stages.
- Riccione’s full scoring matrix has not been reconstructed.
- Rimini has not yet received the same criterion-level QEN mapping used for
  Albenga.
- No offers, evaluation minutes, awards or implementation reports have
  been compared.
- Source counts and criterion counts are not measures of quality.
