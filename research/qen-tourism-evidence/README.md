## Purpose

The Tourism Evidence Assessment studies how claims made by tourism,
hospitality and coastal-service organisations can be transformed into
traceable evidence for human decision-making.

The assessment follows the chain:

> DECLARED → OBSERVABLE → DOCUMENTED → VERIFIED

It does not treat visibility, documentation or self-declaration as proof.

## Scope

Potential applications include:

- hotels and accommodation facilities;
- restaurants and hospitality services;
- beach establishments and coastal operators;
- tourism associations and destination organisations;
- municipalities and public bodies managing tourism-related procedures.

## Evidence stages

### 1. DECLARED

A statement made by the assessed organisation or by a connected party.

Examples: website claims, policy statements, social posts and
self-assessment answers.

A declared claim is not independently verified.

### 2. OBSERVABLE

A condition that can be directly observed through a public interface,
physical inspection or reproducible test.

Observation must record date, method and limitations.

### 3. DOCUMENTED

A claim supported by an identifiable document, record, dataset,
certificate, contract, procedure or official act.

Document existence does not automatically prove implementation.

### 4. VERIFIED

Evidence checked through a reproducible method, an independent source,
cross-source consistency or a documented audit procedure.

Verification must state scope, uncertainty and reviewer responsibility.

## Assessment principles

1. Separate claim, source, evidence and interpretation.
2. Preserve source origin and retrieval date.
3. Record contradictions and missing evidence.
4. Distinguish self-produced evidence from independent evidence.
5. Do not infer compliance from marketing language.
6. Do not infer operational performance from certification alone.
7. Keep legal compliance separate from merit assessment.
8. Require human responsibility for consequential decisions.
9. Minimise personal and commercially sensitive data.
10. Maintain an auditable decision trail.
